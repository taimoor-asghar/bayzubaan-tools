Building compounds: the forward skill

Our compound splitter takes long German words apart. This tool does the reverse: it puts them together. Given two simple roots and a linking sound, you assemble a real German compound — choosing the article from the head and checking the result against 40 verified recipes. Splitting is the reading skill; building is the writing skill. Together they make compounds stop being the scary part of German vocabulary and start being the fun part.

Every compound is a three-part recipe: modifier + linking sound + head. Krank (modifier) + en (linking sound) + Haus (head) = Krankenhaus. Arbeit + s + Zeit = Arbeitszeit. Hand + (nothing) + Schuh = Handschuh. The modifier narrows the meaning; the head sets the category, the gender, and the article. Try the builder above with the defaults first — krank + en + Haus — then click any of the 40 recipes to load it.

The head decides everything

The single most important rule of building compounds: the head (last part) decides the article. Das Haustier is neuter because das Tier is neuter — a pet is fundamentally an animal that lives in a house. Der Handschuh is masculine because der Schuh is — a glove is a kind of shoe. Die Arbeitszeit is feminine because die Zeit is. The builder shows this explicitly: pick any head and the article follows automatically. In the quiz, every third round tests exactly this — which article? — with the head named as a hint. Internalise it and compound gender becomes free: you never memorise a compound's gender, you just know the head's.

Which linking sound? A practical guide

The Fugenlaut (linking sound) sits between the parts, and learners agonise over choosing it. The honest news: there is no perfect rule — natives just know. But there are strong tendencies worth learning: (no sound) is the most common choice overall (Handschuh, Flugzeug, Apfelbaum); -s- follows nouns ending in -heit, -keit, -ung, -schaft (Arbeitszeit, Lebensmittel) and appears in many fixed compounds (Geburtstag); -n- follows feminine nouns ending in -e (Wochenende, Treppenhaus); -en- follows weak masculine nouns and plurals (Krankenhaus, Krankenschwester); -er- follows -er plurals (Kindergarten). The builder's dropdown shows these hints as you pick. When you are unsure in real writing, mimic a known parallel: if Arbeitszeit takes -s-, Arbeitsplatz probably does too.

From building to guessing: why this trains reading

Building compounds backwards-trains the skill you need when reading. Once you have assembled Wasser + Bett yourself, you will never again stare blankly at Wasserbett in a text — your brain pattern-matches it to the recipe. The quiz's assemble rounds exploit this: the distractors are the exact mistakes learners make, Krankhaus (dropped Fugenlaut), Hauskranken (reversed order), and the uncapitalised-head trap Krankenhaus with a lowercase h — German nouns are always capitalised, and the lowercase version is simply not a word. Getting these wrong in the quiz is cheap; getting them wrong on a Fachsprachenprüfung form is not.

Building medical German: the clinical payoff

Medical German is compound-dense, and the builder is a rehearsal space for the vocabulary of the Fachsprachenprüfung. Load Krankenversicherung and note the recipe: krank + en + Versicherung, feminine because die Versicherung is. The same pattern generates Krankmeldung (sick note), Krankengymnastik (physiotherapy), Krankenakte (patient file). Blut + Druck + Messung (blood pressure measurement) shows three-part building: Blutdruck is itself a compound that becomes the modifier. Schmerz + Mittel (painkiller), Fieber + Thermometer, Herz + Rhythmus — the clinic runs on these. Practice building ten medical compounds in the tool, then verify each in the splitter: the round trip (build → split → build) locks the pattern in.

When the literal meaning lies

The builder shows a literal gloss for every creation — but the literal gloss is a scaffold, not the definition. Handschuh is a glove, not footwear; Kindergarten is a preschool, not a garden; Schadenfreude is malicious joy, not damage-happiness; Milchstraße is the Milky Way, not a street made of milk. The builder flags this honestly: a grammatically perfect combination may not be a real word at all, and a real word may not mean what its parts suggest. That is why every recipe carries its actual meaning alongside the recipe — build the form, then check the meaning. This two-step habit (form first, meaning second) is exactly how professionals handle unfamiliar compounds in patient documents.

Drill routine

Day one: load all 40 recipes into the builder one by one and read each recipe aloud — modifier, linking sound, head, article. Day two: use free-build mode to invent ten compounds, then check which of them are real words (the builder tells you when a creation matches the verified list). Day three: run the 15-round quiz until you score 13+/15 twice, paying special attention to the article rounds — the head-decides rule must become automatic. Then take it to the wild: whenever you meet a new compound in a text, mentally build it before you split it. Within weeks you will start producing compounds in speech — the moment German stops feeling like a foreign language and starts feeling like Lego.

Frequently asked questions

How do you build a German compound word?

Combine modifier + linking sound (Fugenlaut) + head: krank + en + Haus = Krankenhaus. The modifier narrows the meaning; the head (last part) sets the article and the core meaning.

Which part of a German compound decides the article?

Always the last part — the head. Das Haustier is neuter because das Tier is neuter; der Handschuh is masculine because der Schuh is. The builder above picks the article from your chosen head automatically.

Do German compounds always need a linking sound?

No — (nothing) is the most common choice (Handschuh, Apfelbaum). When one is needed, -s-, -n-, -en-, -e-, or -er- are the options; the builder's dropdown shows which contexts favour each.

How do I know if a compound I built is a real word?

The builder checks your creation against 40 verified recipes and tells you. A grammatically perfect combination can still be non-existent — always verify an invented compound in a dictionary before using it.

Why is Krankenhaus neuter?

Because its head is das Haus. The head decides the gender: Krankenhaus = a kind of Haus. The same rule gives der Handschuh (head: der Schuh) and die Arbeitszeit (head: die Zeit).

What are the most common Fugenlaute (linking sounds)?

-s- (Arbeitszeit, Geburtstag), -n- (Wochenende), -en- (Krankenhaus), -e- (Hundehütte), -er- (Kindergarten). Natives choose by feel; learners should mimic known parallels rather than deriving rules.
