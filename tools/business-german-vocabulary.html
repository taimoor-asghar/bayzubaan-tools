Business German is a register, not just vocabulary

German business language is a distinct register — formal, precise, and heavily formulaic. The good news: because it is formulaic, it is learnable fast. Emails open with Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren or Sehr geehrte Frau Schmidt, close with Mit freundlichen Grüßen, and in between run on a small set of standard phrases: Im Anhang finden Sie…, Ich bitte um Rückmeldung bis…, Vielen Dank im Voraus. Learn these once and every business email you ever write in German is 80% assembled. The 48 terms in this tool are the building blocks of that register, grouped by where you meet them: meetings, emails, negotiations, the office, and finance.

The single most important rule: in business, it is always Sie. German companies are loosening up — startups use du — but until someone explicitly offers the du, stay with Sie. Getting this wrong is the fastest way to look unprofessional. The second rule: write complete sentences. German business culture reads short, telegram-style emails as rude. Bitte um Rückmeldung. is wrong; Ich bitte um Ihre Rückmeldung bis Freitag. is right. Politeness lives in the full sentence.

Meetings: the language of decisions

Meetings run on a predictable script: die Tagesordnung (agenda), der Vorschlag (proposal), die Abstimmung (vote), der Beschluss (resolution), das Protokoll (minutes). Know these five and you can follow any meeting's structure even when the details escape you. Two pairs to separate: die Entscheidung is the decision itself; der Beschluss is the formal resolution recorded in the minutes — the decision made official. And der Abgabetermin is the deadline: Deadline is used colloquially, but in formal writing use Abgabetermin or Frist.

Practical phrases for participating: Darf ich einen Vorschlag machen? (May I make a suggestion?), Ich stimme zu (I agree), Ich bin dagegen (I am against it), Können wir das vertagen? (Can we postpone this?). You do not need perfect grammar to contribute — you need the ten framing phrases, and this tool's examples show them in context.

E-Mails: the highest-stakes text you write

The German business email has a fixed anatomy. Subject line (der Betreff): short, factual — Angebot Q3 / Rückfrage Rechnung 1042. Salutation: Sehr geehrte Frau Müller (never Liebe Frau Müller in first contact). Body: state the matter, state what you need, state the deadline. Closing: Mit freundlichen Grüßen (or Freundliche Grüße, slightly warmer). Attachments are announced with Im Anhang finden Sie… — never send an attachment unannounced.

Common errors from English speakers: closing with Tschüss or LG (fine for friends, fatal in business); writing Hi as the salutation (use Guten Tag Frau Müller at minimum); forgetting that Sie is capitalised mid-sentence (Ich danke Ihnen, not ihnen). The quiz in this tool drills the fixed phrases — Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren, Mit freundlichen Grüßen, Vielen Dank im Voraus — because these are the ones you must produce perfectly, every time, without thinking.

Verhandlungen: contracts and money talk

Negotiation vocabulary is where precision pays: das Angebot (offer), die Bedingung (condition), der Kompromiss (compromise), der Vertrag (contract), die Unterschrift (signature), die Kündigung (termination — of a contract or an employee). Note that die Kündigung covers both firing and cancelling: context tells you which. Die Rechnung is the invoice; die Zahlung the payment; der Rabatt the discount. Die Rechnung ist innerhalb von 14 Tagen fällig — payment terms are stated explicitly in German business, always check the Zahlungsbedingungen.

Useful negotiation frames: Unter diesen Bedingungen können wir zustimmen (Under these conditions we can agree), Das ist für uns nicht akzeptabel (That is not acceptable for us), Können Sie uns entgegenkommen? (Can you meet us halfway?). German negotiation style is direct — indirectness reads as evasiveness. Say what you mean, in complete sentences, with the terms from this tool.

Büro and Finanzen: the daily and the numerical

Office life: der Kollege / die Kollegin, der Vorgesetzte (supervisor — literally "the one placed above"), die Abteilung, die Personalabteilung (HR). Career vocabulary clusters here too: die Bewerbung (application), das Vorstellungsgespräch (interview — literally "introduction conversation"), die Gehaltsverhandlung (salary negotiation), der Urlaubsantrag (vacation request). In Germany vacation is requested in writing and approved in writing — der Urlaubsantrag wurde genehmigt is a sentence you will personally live.

Finance terms are mostly transparent if you know the components: der Umsatz (revenue/turnover), der Gewinn (profit), der Verlust (loss), die Steuer (tax), das Budget, die Kosten, die Investition, die Bilanz (balance sheet). Watch the articles — der Gewinn, der Verlust, die Steuer — and note that die Kosten is always plural in this sense. Drill each term with its article as a single unit, exactly as the cards present them.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use du or Sie in German business emails?

Always Sie until explicitly offered du. Start with Sehr geehrte/r + surname, close with Mit freundlichen Grüßen. Startups may use du, but let them offer it first.

What is the difference between die Entscheidung and der Beschluss?

Die Entscheidung is the decision itself; der Beschluss is the formal resolution recorded in the minutes — the decision made official and minuted.

Is it okay to write 'Hi' or 'Tschüss' in a German business email?

No. Use Guten Tag Frau/Herr + surname at minimum for the salutation, and Mit freundlichen Grüßen to close. Tschüss and LG are for friends, not business.

What is the difference between die Kündigung as firing vs cancelling?

Same word, both meanings — context tells you which. Die Kündigung des Vertrags is cancelling the contract; die Kündigung des Mitarbeiters is firing the employee.

How do I announce an attachment in German?

With Im Anhang finden Sie… (Please find attached…). Never send an attachment without mentioning it in the body.

What does 'Ich setze Sie in Kopie' mean?

I am putting you in cc (copy). In Kopie (cc) and blinde Kopie (bcc) work exactly like their English equivalents.
