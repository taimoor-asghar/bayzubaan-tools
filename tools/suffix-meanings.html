Suffixes: German's gender machines

German noun gender feels random until you learn the suffixes — then huge swathes of it become rule-governed. The four suffixes in this tool — -heit, -keit, -ung, -schaft — are always feminine: die Krankheit, die Möglichkeit, die Prüfung, die Freundschaft, no exceptions. That's hundreds of nouns whose gender you never need to memorise. Suffixes do double duty: they tell you the gender and the meaning pattern. -ung on a verb stem names the action or its result (prüfen → Prüfung); -schaft on a noun names the collective (Freund → Freundschaft). Learn the four patterns and unknown long nouns start decoding themselves — the ending tells you what kind of thing the word is.

-heit vs -keit: which one?

The classic learner question: why Krankheit but Möglichkeit? The rule of thumb: -keit follows adjectives ending in -ig, -lich, -bar, -sam, -isch (möglich → Möglichkeit, höflich → Höflichkeit, dankbar → Dankbarkeit, wirklich → Wirklichkeit); -heit takes most others (krank → Krankheit, frei → Freiheit, schön → Schönheit). There are historical exceptions (Neuigkeit, not *Neuheit), but the rule covers the vast majority. Both mean "the state/quality of": -heit/-keit turns a description into a thing. When guessing a noun from an adjective, try -keit first if the adjective ends in -ig/-lich/-bar, -heit otherwise — you'll be right most of the time.

-ung: the verb nominalizer (exam gold)

-ung turns verb stems into nouns naming the action or its result, and it's everywhere in formal and professional German. Prüfen → Prüfung (exam), behandeln → Behandlung (treatment), untersuchen → Untersuchung (examination), ausbilden → Ausbildung (training), überweisen → Überweisung (referral) — the vocabulary of universities, hospitals, and bureaucracy runs on -ung. Note the stem changes: the -ung noun uses the verb's stem, sometimes with umlaut or consonant shifts inherited from strong verbs (fahren → Erfahrung is irregular — memorise it). In writing, -ung nouns let you compress clauses: die Behandlung des Patienten instead of dass der Patient behandelt wird — the nominal style (Nominalstil) that German officialdom loves and learners must at least decode.

-schaft: collectives and bonds

-schaft (cognate with English -ship: friendship = Freundschaft) builds three kinds of nouns. Collectives: Mann → Mannschaft (team), verwandt → Verwandtschaft (relatives). Relationships: Freund → Freundschaft, Partner → Partnerschaft. Fields and states: wissen → Wissenschaft (science — "knowledge-ship"), Wirt → Wirtschaft (economy — from the innkeeper's household!), bereit → Bereitschaft (readiness — and Bereitschaftsdienst, on-call duty, a word every medical worker in Germany knows). The English -ship cognate is a handy bridge: where English has -ship, German often has -schaft.

Suffixes in medical German

Medical German is a suffix showcase, and the Fachsprachenprüfung assumes you can decode it live. -heit: Krankheit (disease), Sicherheit (safety — Patientensicherheit is a whole discipline). -keit: Wirklichkeit less so, but Übelkeit (nausea — from übel!) is daily clinical vocabulary. -ung: the entire clinical workflow — Untersuchung, Behandlung, Überweisung, Einweisung (admission), Entlassung (discharge). -schaft: Bereitschaft (on-call), Wissenschaft (the literature you should be reading). Notice the pattern: the suffix tells you the word's job (-ung = process, -heit = state), so even an unknown -ung word in a discharge letter is recognisable as some process — and the verb stem inside names which one. That's the decoding skill this tool builds.

Suffix stacking: word chains

Suffixes stack, and the chains are where advanced vocabulary lives. Wissen (know) → Wissenschaft (science, via -schaft) → wissenschaftlich (scientific, via -lich) → Wissenschaftler (scientist, via -er). Freund → freundlich → Freundlichkeit. Arbeit → arbeiten → Arbeiter → arbeitslos → Arbeitslosigkeit. Each link is predictable if you know the affixes — and each link's gender follows its final suffix (die Freundlichkeit, der Wissenschaftler). Reading a stacked word? Peel from the right: the last suffix sets gender and word class, then work leftwards. Arbeitslosigkeit = -keit (feminine state noun) ← arbeitslos ← Arbeit: "the state of being without work." Five seconds, no dictionary — and this exact peeling skill is what the compound splitter (tool 41) trains for compounds. Suffixes and compounds together decode nearly every long German word.

More suffixes worth knowing

The big four aren't alone. -tion (always feminine): Information, Operation, Station — Latinate, often medical. -ik (feminine): Musik, Klinik, Technik. -ur (feminine): Kultur, Natur, Klausur. -anz/-enz (feminine): Distanz, Existenz, Tendenz. -ismus (masculine): Tourismus, Realismus. -ling (masculine): Lehrling (apprentice), Liebling (darling). -chen/-lein (neuter, diminutive): Mädchen, Häuschen — always neuter, even das Mädchen for a girl. -in (feminine): Ärztin, Lehrerin — the feminine person suffix. -er (masculine): Lehrer, Berliner, Computer — people and machines. Notice the pattern: most abstract suffixes are feminine, most person suffixes follow natural gender. Between this list and the big four, you can gender-guess the majority of German nouns you'll ever meet.

The decoding strategy

Meet an unknown long noun? Run this checklist: 1. Strip the suffix — is it -heit/-keit/-ung/-schaft? If yes, gender is die, free. 2. Find the base: adjective (-heit/-keit), verb (-ung), or noun (-schaft). 3. Compose: base meaning + suffix pattern (bereit "ready" + -schaft "state" = readiness). 4. Sanity-check against the sentence. This works on the vast majority of suffixed nouns and turns dictionary lookups into confirmations rather than discoveries. Drill routine: browse one suffix per day in the explorer, saying each base → noun pair aloud with its article (die, always die). Then run the 15-round quiz to 12+/15 twice — the suffix-identification rounds (which suffix builds this?) train the pattern, not the word. Within a month, -ung nouns will feel like old friends.

Frequently asked questions

What gender are German nouns ending in -heit, -keit, -ung, -schaft?

Always feminine (die) — no exceptions. Die Krankheit, die Möglichkeit, die Prüfung, die Freundschaft. These four suffixes are the most reliable gender rule in German.

When do I use -heit vs -keit?

Use -keit after adjectives ending in -ig, -lich, -bar, -sam, -isch (möglich → Möglichkeit, höflich → Höflichkeit); -heit for most others (krank → Krankheit, frei → Freiheit).

What does the suffix -ung do?

It nominalises verbs — turns the verb stem into a noun naming the action or its result: prüfen → Prüfung (exam), behandeln → Behandlung (treatment), untersuchen → Untersuchung (examination).

What does -schaft mean?

Cognate with English -ship: it builds collectives (Mannschaft = team), relationships (Freundschaft), and fields/states (Wissenschaft = science, Bereitschaft = readiness/on-call duty).

Why is it 'die Überweisung' for a medical referral?

Überweisen (to transfer/refer) + -ung (the action/result) = the referral — feminine because all -ung nouns are feminine. The same pattern gives Einweisung (admission) and Entlassung (discharge).

How do I decode an unknown German noun with a suffix?

Strip the suffix (gender is die), find the base (adjective for -heit/-keit, verb for -ung, noun for -schaft), then compose: base meaning + suffix pattern. Check against the sentence.
