    The anatomy of a German business email

    A formal German email follows a fixed skeleton that examiners, employers, and officials all expect: Betreff (subject), Anrede (salutation), Einleitung (opening), Anliegen (the matter itself), Bitte (the request), Dank (thanks), Grußformel (sign-off), Signatur (signature block). This tool gives you ten frames — one per section — that compose into a complete email. Fill the slots, add the sentences to your letter, copy it, and the structure is handled. What the tool cannot do is choose your words: the prose below teaches the register rules behind every slot, so the email sounds written by you, not assembled by a machine.

    The governing principle is Höflichkeit durch Distanz — politeness through distance. German formality is not warmth; it is respectful distance kept through the Sie-form, the Konjunktiv II, and fixed formulas. Ich möchte Sie bitten, … (I would like to ask you) instead of Bitte tun Sie … (Please do …). Ich wäre Ihnen dankbar, wenn Sie … könnten (I would be grateful if you could) instead of Können Sie …? The grammar is doing social work: every Konjunktiv II puts a layer of optionality between you and the reader, which is exactly what a German office expects from a stranger.

    Betreff and Anrede: the two most-scrutinised lines

    The Betreff is the email's title and, in German offices, part of the record: Betreff: Terminanfrage — Dr. Müller. State the matter in two to five words, add a reference number for applications (Bewerbung als Assistenzarzt — Kennziffer 24/2026), and for replies reference the earlier mail (Rückfrage zu Ihrer E-Mail vom 12. Oktober). Never leave the subject empty — an empty Betreff reads as either spam or carelessness, and many offices sort mail by it.

    The Anrede decides the grammar of everything after it: Sehr geehrte Frau Schmidt, — with the comma — means the email continues in lowercase (ich wende mich an Sie …), because the salutation is the start of the sentence, not a standalone line. This is the rule most learners miss: a comma after the salutation, then lowercase. Get the endings right — geehrte Frau (feminine accusative), geehrter Herr (masculine nominative-as-address), geehrte Damen und Herren (the safe plural when no name is known). The quiz tests exactly these endings, because a wrong ending in the Anrede is visible before a single content word is read.

    Einleitung and Anliegen: state who you are, say why you write

    After the salutation, German emails waste no time: ich wende mich an Sie, mit einer Frage zu Ihrem Angebot. The opening names the reason in one clause — mit einer Frage zu …, in der Angelegenheit meiner Bewerbung, da ich mich für den Kurs anmelden möchte. Introductions follow only if the reader cannot know you: Mein Name ist Anna Schmidt und ich studiere Medizin im vierten Semester. Note the verb-second order in ich studiere Medizin im vierten Semester — the dative im vierten Semester sits after the verb, never before it. A common error is fronting the time phrase and forgetting the inversion (und im vierten Semester studiere ich Medizin — the correct inverted form, if you do front it).

    Konkret geht es um … — the Anliegen frame — narrows the general reason to the specific point: die Anmeldefrist bis zum 15. November, die beigefügte Bescheinigung. This sentence is where vagueness dies. A German official reading your email wants the actionable noun in the first paragraph: the deadline, the document, the appointment. Bury it in paragraph three and the reply will ask what you wanted — costing you a week.

    The Bitte: asking without commanding

    The request frame carries the heaviest politeness machinery: Ich möchte Sie bitten, mir einen Termin in der kommenden Woche zu geben. Three devices work together. First, möchte — Konjunktiv II of mögen, softening the verb itself. Second, the infinitive-with-zu chain mir … zu geben, which keeps the request grammatical rather than imperative. Third, the dative mir before the accusative object — mir einen Termin geben, never mich einen Termin or the dropped-mir variant. Learners who say Ich will Sie bitten have changed the register entirely: wollen is a demand, möchten a wish, and German offices hear the difference.

    The strongest single formula is the conditional gratitude frame: Ich wäre Ihnen dankbar, wenn Sie mir bis Ende der Woche antworten könnten. The grammar is wäre (Konjunktiv II of sein) plus könnten (Konjunktiv II of können) — double softening, and it sets a deadline without giving an order. Compare it with Antworten Sie mir bitte bis Ende der Woche, which is what a superior writes to a subordinate, not what a stranger writes to an office. The distractor in the quiz that changes könnten to können tests exactly this: one vowel of politeness.

    Attachments, thanks, and the sign-off

    Im Anhang finden Sie meinen Lebenslauf. German emails announce attachments explicitly — the reader should never have to discover them. Note the accusative meinen Lebenslauf (masculine): the most common article error in the whole email happens right here, in the sentence people write fastest. Vielen Dank im Voraus für Ihre schnelle Rückmeldung — the thanks frame — keeps Vielen Dank plural (viel Dank is a different, thinner phrase) and matches the adjective ending to the feminine noun: Ihre schnelle Rückmeldung.

    The Grußformel has a strict hierarchy: Mit freundlichen Grüßen is the neutral standard for all business; Mit besten Grüßen is slightly warmer but still formal; Hochachtungsvoll is reserved for complaints and the highest formality — using it for a normal enquiry sounds archaic. And never translate English closings literally: there is no German mit lieben Grüßen in business mail. The signature block — name, position, phone, address — sits below the sign-off as plain text; offices print emails, and a signature that only exists as a logo image is lost.

    Common traps: the seven errors that mark a learner

    One: capitalising after the salutation's comma — the email must continue lowercase (ich wende mich …, not Ich wende …). Two: du leaking into a formal mail — every pronoun is Sie, every possessive Ihr; the gate below checks the entire composed corpus for leaks. Three: Hi or Hallo as the salutation — Hallo Frau Schmidt is colloquial; in a first contact it reads as disrespectful. Four: ending with LG or VG — abbreviations of the sign-off belong in chats, never in a first formal email. Five: the wrong geehrte/geehrter ending. Six: Ich will instead of Ich möchte. Seven: forgetting the Betreff entirely. Run through this list before sending any real email — and drill the quiz until the distractors, which are all built from these seven errors, stop fooling you.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the correct order of a formal German email?

    Betreff (subject), Anrede (salutation with comma), Einleitung (reason for writing), Anliegen (the specific matter), Bitte (the polite request), Dank (thanks), Grußformel (sign-off), Signatur (name, position, contact).

    Do I capitalise the first word after the salutation?

    No — the salutation ends with a comma, and the email continues lowercase: Sehr geehrte Frau Schmidt, — ich wende mich an Sie … The salutation is the start of the sentence.

    How do I ask for something politely in a German email?

    Use double softening: Ich möchte Sie bitten, … (I would like to ask you) or the conditional gratitude formula Ich wäre Ihnen dankbar, wenn Sie … könnten (I would be grateful if you could). Never use wollen or the imperative with a stranger.

    Mit freundlichen Grüßen or Hochachtungsvoll — which sign-off?

    Mit freundlichen Grüßen is the neutral standard for all business mail. Hochachtungsvoll is reserved for complaints and the highest formality; for a normal enquiry it sounds archaic.

    How do I mention an attachment in German?

    Announce it explicitly: Im Anhang finden Sie meinen Lebenslauf. Note the accusative — meinen Lebenslauf (masculine) is the most common article error in German business emails.

    What should the Betreff (subject) contain?

    The matter in two to five words — Betreff: Terminanfrage — Dr. Müller — plus a reference number for applications (Kennziffer 24/2026) and the date of the earlier mail for replies. Never leave it empty.
