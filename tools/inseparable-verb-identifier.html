Separable vs. inseparable: the two prefix tribes

German verb prefixes belong to two tribes with opposite behaviour. Separable prefixes (ab-, an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, vor-, zu-, zurück- …) detach in main clauses: Ich rufe an. Inseparable prefixes never detach: Ich besuche dich. Ich verstehe das. Confusing the tribes produces the most audible learner errors in German — saying ich besuche dich is fine, but ich suche dich be is word salad, and ich habe dich begesucht doubles the mistake.

The inseparable tribe is small and closed. Memorise it once and every other prefix defaults to separable:

  • be- — makes verbs transitive, often "providing with": besuchen (visit), beschreiben (describe), bekommen (receive), bezahlen (pay), beobachten (observe).
  • ge- — stative/resultative: gefallen (please), gehören (belong), gelingen (succeed), geschehen (happen).
  • er- — achieving, obtaining: erzählen (tell), erfahren (learn/find out), erwarten (expect), erreichen (reach).
  • ver- — the busiest prefix: wrongness (verstehen? no — ver- covers change, loss, mistake): verlieren (lose), vergessen (forget), verkaufen (sell), verbessern (improve), verordnen (prescribe).
  • zer- — apart, to pieces: zerbrechen (shatter), zerstören (destroy), zerteilen (divide).
  • ent- — away, removal, beginning: entscheiden (decide), entfernen (remove), entstehen (arise), entdecken (discover).
  • emp- — variant of ent-: empfehlen (recommend), empfangen (receive), empfinden (feel).
  • miss- — failure, wrongness: missverstehen (misunderstand), misslingen (fail).

The ge- test and the stress test

Two diagnostics settle any verb. The ge- test: form the Partizip II. If ge- appears, the prefix is separable (angerufen); if not, inseparable (besucht, verstanden). The stress test: separable prefixes carry the stress (ANrufen), inseparable prefixes are unstressed (beSUchen). Native speakers do this unconsciously; learners can do it deliberately.

A handful of prefixes live in both tribes — durch-, über-, unter-, um-, wider- — and stress plus meaning decide: UMfahren (separable, to knock down) vs. umFAHren (inseparable, to drive around); ÜBERsetzen (separable, to ferry across) vs. überSETzen (inseparable, to translate). The everyday meaning is usually the separable one; the abstract or figurative meaning is usually inseparable.

Why this matters for medical German

The Fachsprachenprüfung runs on inseparable verbs. The entire clinical vocabulary of doing things to patients is be-/ver-/ent-/unter- territory: untersuchen (examine), behandeln (treat), beschreiben (describe symptoms), verordnen (prescribe), besprechen (discuss), entlassen (discharge), überweisen (refer), vermeiden (avoid), entscheiden (decide). A candidate who separates these — ich untersuche den Patienten is correct; ich suche den Patienten unter is a disaster — fails the language impression in the first minute. Conversely, everyday ward verbs are separable: aufnehmen (admit), absetzen (discontinue), einnehmen (take medication), aufklären (inform/consent).

Notice the pattern: inseparable prefixes tend to make verbs more abstract and transitive — exactly the register of professional documentation. Separable prefixes stay concrete and directional — the register of bedside action. Knowing which tribe a verb belongs to is therefore not just grammar; it is register awareness.

Frequently asked questions

Which German prefixes are always inseparable?

be-, ge-, er-, ver-, zer-, ent-, emp- and miss- never separate: besuchen, gefallen, erzählen, verstehen, zerbrechen, entscheiden, empfehlen, missverstehen. Every other prefix defaults to separable.

How can you tell if a verb is separable or inseparable?

Two tests: the ge- test (separable verbs put ge- between prefix and stem in the participle: angerufen; inseparable verbs take no ge-: besucht) and the stress test (separable prefixes are stressed: ANrufen; inseparable prefixes are not: beSUchen).

Is 'umfahren' separable or inseparable?

Both — stress decides. UMfahren (separable) means to knock down; umFAHren (inseparable) means to drive around. The same duality exists for durch-, über-, unter- and wider-.

Why is it 'besucht' and not 'gebesucht'?

The prefix be- is inseparable, and inseparable-prefix verbs never take ge- in the Partizip II: besucht, verstanden, erzählt. Adding ge- is one of the most common learner mistakes.

Does the prefix separate in 'Ich besuche dich'?

No — besuchen has the inseparable prefix be-, so it behaves like a normal verb: Ich besuche dich, ich habe dich besucht. Only separable prefixes (an-, auf-, ab- …) detach: Ich rufe dich an.
