The German Lebenslauf: a document with its own laws

The German Lebenslauf is not a résumé. It is a tabular, reverse-chronological, gapless, signed and dated record of a life — and German recruiters read it with expectations no other country's CV carries. This tool teaches the 40 words and phrases that build it: the section headers (Persönliche Daten, Berufserfahrung, Ausbildung), the personal-data fields, the experience verbs, the qualification nouns, and the descriptor adjectives. Each entry ships with a German example sentence and its English translation, plus a 15-round mixed-direction quiz. The prose below teaches the document the vocabulary belongs to — because the right word in the wrong format still fails.

The three laws of the Lebenslauf: tabellarisch (tabular — two columns, dates left, content right), lückenlos (gapless — every month since school accounted for; a six-month gap needs an entry like berufliche Orientierung), and unterschrieben (signed and dated at the bottom — an unsigned Lebenslauf is technically incomplete). Reverse chronological order is mandatory: the most recent position first, the Abitur last. German recruiters scan top-down, and the first three lines decide whether the rest is read.

Section headers: the skeleton

Every German CV carries the same headers, in this order: Persönliche Daten (personal details), Berufserfahrung (professional experience), Ausbildung (education — note: in Germany Ausbildung covers both university study and vocational training), Weiterbildung (further training — courses, certificates, the proof of lifelong learning), Kenntnisse (skills — IT, specialist knowledge, driving licence), Sprachkenntnisse (language skills — graded by the CEFR levels A1–C2, never by vague fließend alone), Interessen (interests — two or three, genuine, no Lesen und Reisen cliché), Referenzen (references — in Germany: auf Anfrage, on request, never with contact details). Learn these eight headers and you can read any German CV; learn to write them and you can build your own.

A note on die Kenntnisse versus die Fähigkeiten: Kenntnisse is knowledge you can name (languages, software, procedures), Fähigkeiten is ability you demonstrate. The CV header is Kenntnisse — the nouns that follow are things like EDV-Kenntnisse and Sprachkenntnisse, and the example sentence Gute EDV-Kenntnisse werden heute überall erwartet shows the plural form recruiters expect.

Personal data: what Germany still asks

The Persönliche Daten block opens with Vorname and Nachname — surname first in the formal German order (Mustermann, Max). Then Geburtsdatum and Geburtsort (date and place of birth — still standard in Germany, unlike in Anglo-American CVs), written as TT.MM.JJJJ: Das Geburtsdatum wird im Format TT.MM.JJJJ geschrieben. Staatsangehörigkeit (nationality) matters for international applicants and for positions with security requirements. Familienstand (marital status) and the children's question are legally optional today — the example notes Der Familienstand ist heute optional — and modern advice is to omit them; the entry stays in the vocabulary because you will still read it on older templates. Adresse and Telefonnummer close the block: both must be current, because the recruiter's first action after reading is calling.

Experience verbs: the action vocabulary

German CVs describe experience with past-tense action verbs, and the verb choice is the difference between a flat list and a career. verantwortlich für (responsible for) is the weightiest: Ich war für ein Team von zehn Mitarbeitern verantwortlich — with war, the completed past, and the accusative team. betreuen (to look after, supervise) is the care professions' signature verb: Ich betreute bis zu 25 Patienten pro Schicht. leiten (to lead), koordinieren (to coordinate), durchführen (to carry out — separable, so Ich führte … durch), entwickeln (to develop), schulen (to train others — not to be confused with studieren), abschließen (to complete — Ich schloss die Ausbildung mit sehr gut ab, with the grade as the payoff). Each entry gives the infinitive and the past participle form because the CV uses the simple past (Präteritum) for completed experience: betreute, leitete, führte … durch.

The trap to avoid: English-style noun-heavy bullet points (Responsible for team leadership) translate badly. The German convention is the verb-first sentence — Ich leitete die Einführung des neuen Systems — because German recruiters read for agency: what did you do. Passive constructions (Es wurde ein System eingeführt) hide the agent and weaken the entry.

Qualifications and descriptors: proof and personality

The qualifications block is where German thoroughness shows: das abgeschlossene Studium (completed degree), die abgeschlossene Berufsausbildung (completed vocational training — the dual system's pride), die Facharztausbildung (specialist training, five to six years), das Praktikum (internship — note the plural Praktika in the example), das Zertifikat (certificate), die Promotion (doctorate — in Germany a Dr. title is used socially and belongs on the CV header if earned). Abgeschlossen (completed) is the key adjective: German CVs distinguish sharply between begonnen and abgeschlossen, and an unfinished qualification must say so honestly — Studium der Medizin (5 Semester) — because the Zeugnisse (certificates) attached will reveal it anyway.

The descriptors — zuverlässig, teamfähig, belastbar, selbstständig, engagiert, flexibel, lernbereit, sorgfältig — are the same inventory as the Bewerbung's strengths section, and the same rule applies: three of them, and each must survive the interview's Beispiel bitte (example, please). Belastbar literally means load-bearing — In der Notaufnahme muss man belastbar sein — and it is the single most-used descriptor in German job adverts. Selbstständig carries the umlaut and the double meaning (independent; also self-employed — context disambiguates). Use the example sentences as templates: Ich arbeite zuverlässig und termintreu shows the descriptor inside a real claim, which is how it belongs on the page.

Frequently asked questions

What sections does a German Lebenslauf have?

In order: Persönliche Daten (personal details), Berufserfahrung (experience), Ausbildung (education), Weiterbildung (further training), Kenntnisse (skills), Sprachkenntnisse (languages), Interessen (interests), Referenzen auf Anfrage (references on request).

What are the three laws of the German CV?

Tabellarisch (tabular, dates left, content right), lückenlos (gapless — every month since school accounted for), unterschrieben (signed and dated). Reverse chronological order, most recent first.

Should I include Geburtsdatum and Familienstand?

Geburtsdatum and Geburtsort are still standard in Germany. Familienstand (marital status) is legally optional today — modern advice is to omit it, though you will still read it on older templates.

How do I describe experience in a German CV?

With past-tense action verbs, verb-first: Ich leitete die Einführung des neuen Systems. Key verbs: verantwortlich für, betreuen, leiten, koordinieren, durchführen, entwickeln, schulen, abschließen. Avoid passive constructions that hide your agency.

What is the difference between Kenntnisse and Fähigkeiten?

Kenntnisse is nameable knowledge (languages, software, procedures) — the CV header. Fähigkeiten is demonstrated ability. List EDV-Kenntnisse and Sprachkenntnisse under Kenntnisse, graded by CEFR level.

Which adjectives describe strengths on a German CV?

The standard inventory: zuverlässig, teamfähig, belastbar, selbstständig, engagiert, flexibel, lernbereit, sorgfältig. Pick three you can defend — the interview will ask for the story behind each one.
