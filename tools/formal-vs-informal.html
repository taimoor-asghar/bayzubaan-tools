du vs Sie: two languages in one

German has two words for "you," and choosing wrong is the fastest way to sound rude — or ridiculous. Du is for friends, family, children, fellow students, teammates, and (increasingly) colleagues. Sie is for strangers, customers, older people, professional contexts, and — critically — patients. The grammar follows the choice: du takes the familiar verb form (kannst, machst, bist), Sie takes the formal one (können, machen, sind) — identical to the sie (they) form, but always capitalised: Sie, Ihnen, Ihre. This tool gives you 30 everyday sentences in both registers plus 10 typical situations, and the quiz drills the two skills that matter: picking the right register, and getting the Sie form exactly right.

The three things that change

Converting du → Sie changes exactly three things. 1. The verb: kannst → können, sprichst → sprechen, nimmst → nehmen, bist → sind — the Sie form always matches the infinitive stem (it's the same as the sie/they form). 2. The pronoun: du → Sie, dich → Sie, dir → Ihnen, dein → Ihr. 3. The capital letter: Sie, Ihnen, Ihrem, Ihre — always capital, even mid-sentence. The classic learner error is *können sie mir helfen? with a lowercase sie — which means "can they help me?" The quiz's distractors are built from exactly these errors, so you'll learn to spot the capital-S at a glance.

When Sie: the safe defaults

When in doubt, use Sie — it's never rude, only occasionally distant. Default to Sie with: anyone visibly over ~30 you don't know, all customers and clients, officials and police, landlords, and every adult patient. In shops, restaurants, offices, and on the phone, Sie is the unmarked choice. The one systematic exception: if someone offers you du ("Wollen wir uns nicht duzen?"), accept gracefully — refusing is colder than the du itself. Note the offer usually comes from the older or higher-ranking person; as the younger/junior party, wait to be offered.

When du: the expanding territory

Du is spreading. Fellow students: du from day one. Colleagues: du in startups, agencies, hospitals, and increasingly everywhere under 40 — many companies now have an explicit du-culture. Clubs, teams, and gyms: du. Children: always du. Fellow travellers your age: usually du. Social media: du. The tricky middle ground is colleagues over 40 in traditional firms — start with Sie, switch when offered or when everyone else has. And there's the Hamburger Sie: Sie + first name ("Können Sie mir helfen, Thomas?") — common among colleagues who aren't quite on du terms. It sounds odd to learners but it's standard professional German.

Sie in the clinic: non-negotiable

For medical German, the rule is absolute: Sie with every adult patient, always. Nehmen Sie regelmäßig Medikamente? Haben Sie Allergien? Beschreiben Sie bitte Ihre Schmerzen. Using du with a patient reads as disrespectful or unprofessional — it's one of the fastest ways to fail the Fachsprachenprüfung's communication section. Among colleagues, the ward usually runs on du (nurses, doctors, therapists), which creates the classic code-switch: du with the team in the corridor, Sie the moment you enter the patient's room. Practice the switch deliberately — the explorer's three doctor–patient pairs are your starter set. And in writing (discharge letters, referrals), Sie-forms are mandatory throughout.

Emails: register in writing

Written German makes the register visible. Sie-emails: Sehr geehrte Frau Müller, … Mit freundlichen Grüßen. Du-emails: Hallo Thomas, … Viele Grüße / Liebe Grüße. Never mix: *Hallo Frau Müller with Sie-verbs is a classic learner blend — pick one lane. Note the vocabulary upgrades that come with Sie: bekommen → erhalten, kriegen → erhalten, fragen → sich erkundigen in very formal contexts. The quiz's email pair (Hast du die E-Mail bekommen? → Haben Sie die E-Mail erhalten?) shows both flips at once.

When you get it wrong: recovery

Everyone mistimes the register sometimes — what matters is the recovery. Accidental du to a Sie-person: a light Entschuldigung! and switch — Germans are forgiving when they hear you're a learner; the accent is your apology in advance. Accidental Sie to a du-person: smile it off — being too formal is never offensive, just distant. Mid-conversation switch: if a colleague says Sag doch du zu mir!, switch immediately and completely — half-switching (du verbs with Sie pronouns) sounds stranger than either. Unsure in the moment: dodge the pronoun entirely — Kann ich helfen? works for both, as do passive constructions (Wird noch etwas gewünscht?). Native speakers use these dodges too when they're unsure. The golden rule of recovery: correct fast, don't over-apologise, and keep talking — fluency forgives everything.

Drill routine

Day one: read all 30 pairs aloud, du then Sie — feel the three changes (verb, pronoun, capital). Day two: cover the Sie column and convert the du sentences yourself before checking. Day three: run the 15-round quiz to 13+/15 twice — the lowercase-sie trap must become instantly visible. Then live practice: narrate your day in Sie-form (Ich habe Sie gestern nicht gesehen…), and rehearse the three clinic sentences until they're automatic. The moment you catch yourself writing sie instead of Sie and fix it without thinking, the register has landed.

Frequently asked questions

When should I use 'du' vs 'Sie' in German?

Du: friends, family, children, fellow students, teammates, and increasingly colleagues. Sie: strangers, customers, older people, professional contexts, and all adult patients. When in doubt, use Sie.

What is the difference between 'sie' and 'Sie'?

Capitalisation: Sie (capital S) is the formal 'you'; sie (lowercase) means 'she' or 'they.' *Können sie mir helfen? means 'can they help me?' — the capital letter carries the politeness.

What is the 'Hamburger Sie'?

Sie + first name ('Können Sie mir helfen, Thomas?') — standard among colleagues who aren't on du terms. It sounds odd to learners but is normal professional German.

Do German doctors use du or Sie with patients?

Always Sie with adult patients — du can feel disrespectful and will cost marks in the Fachsprachenprüfung. Staff typically use du among themselves.

How do I offer someone 'du' in German?

Say 'Wollen wir uns nicht duzen?' (Shall we use du?). The offer usually comes from the older or higher-ranking person; as the junior party, wait to be offered.

Is 'du' becoming more common in German workplaces?

Yes — startups, agencies, hospitals, and under-40 workplaces increasingly default to du, and many companies declare an explicit du-culture. Traditional firms and banks still start with Sie.
