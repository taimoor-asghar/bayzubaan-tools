Psychiatric German: where every word is a clinical act

In psychiatry, language is not decoration — it is the intervention. The words you choose can open a patient up or shut them down, and the Fachsprachenprüfung tests whether your German carries that awareness. Examiners listen for the vocabulary of mental health delivered with clinical sensitivity: correct terms, person-first phrasing, and the conversation sentences that make disclosure possible. This tool gives you 32 cards across four categories — diagnoses, symptoms, treatment, conversation — each with a sensitivity note, plus a 12-round quiz that tests both translation and judgment: which wording is clinically appropriate?

Start with the diagnoses, because naming is the first clinical act. Die Depression, die Angststörung, die bipolare Störung, die Schizophrenie, die posttraumatische Belastungsstörung, die Essstörung, die Zwangsstörung, die Persönlichkeitsstörung — all feminine, all ending in -störung or -ie, all standard. Two notes examiners care about: use Störung (disorder) rather than Krankheit where the standard term uses it, and never use a diagnosis as a label for a person. Not der Schizophrene — der Mensch mit Schizophrenie. Person-first language is not political correctness in the exam; it is scored as professional communication.

Suizid, not Selbstmord: the vocabulary of risk

The single most important lexical choice in psychiatric German: Suizid, never Selbstmord. Selbstmord (self-murder) carries moral condemnation; Suizid is the neutral clinical term, and Freitod romanticises it. The standard question is Haben Sie schon einmal daran gedacht, sich etwas anzutun? — sich etwas antun (to harm oneself) is the gentle clinical phrasing; sich umbringen is what you never say to a patient. Then the direct follow-up: Haben Sie einen Plan? Asked plainly, without euphemism — risk assessment requires directness, and examiners mark candidates who dodge it.

The symptom cards give you the vocabulary around that conversation: die Suizidgedanken (always plural in German — Suizidgedanken haben), die Halluzination (ask Hören Sie Stimmen, die andere nicht hören?), der Wahn (with the adjective wahnhaft), die Antriebslosigkeit (lack of drive — the depression symptom to ask about with concrete Alltag examples), die Schlafstörung (distinguish Einschlaf- from Durchschlafstörungen), die Panikattacke (note: Attacke, not Anfall, in psychiatric context), die Stimmungsschwankung (neutral — never launisch to a patient), das Trauma (use traumatisiert carefully; it labels).

Treatment terms and the discharge that matters

The treatment cards cover the care pathway: die Psychotherapie (generic — specify Verhaltenstherapie, often VT in notes, when you mean CBT), das Antidepressivum (plural Antidepressiva — and always tell the patient the effect takes weeks), die Krisenintervention (name the local Krisendienst by name), die stationäre Behandlung (softer and more standard than Einweisung), die Medikation, die Selbsthilfegruppe (name one locally at discharge), die Nachsorge (arrange it before the patient leaves — a discharge without Nachsorge is an incomplete discharge).

Notice the exam logic: psychiatric scenarios are scored on the plan as much as the words. The candidate who says Wir vereinbaren die Nachsorge und ich gebe Ihnen die Nummer des Krisendienstes mit demonstrates clinical thinking in German, not just vocabulary. The cards' sensitivity notes are your shortcut to that register.

The conversation: sentences that make disclosure possible

The eight conversation cards are the heart of the tool, because psychiatric exams are scored on the alliance you build. Möchten Sie darüber sprechen? — offers, never forces, disclosure. Ich nehme das sehr ernst. — validate before moving on; say it the moment risk is disclosed. Das ist keine Schwäche. — counters the stigma the patient walked in with. Sie sind nicht allein damit. — normalises help-seeking, always paired with a concrete next step. Fühlen Sie sich sicher zu Hause? — screens for domestic risk without accusation. Wir finden gemeinsam eine Lösung. — gemeinsam is the alliance word; the exam rewards the doctor who treats with the patient, not the patient.

All eight use the Sie-form with zero du-leakage, and all eight are short — psychiatric patients in crisis cannot process complex sentences, and the exam simulates exactly that constraint. Drill them until they come out calm and unhurried: speed is for the emergency tool; here the skill is steadiness.

How to drill: the sensitivity round

Study the cards by category, tapping each to reveal the English and the sensitivity note — read the note, not just the translation, because the note is what the examiner scores. Then take the quiz: eight translation items and four judgment items (Suizid vs. Selbstmord, person-first phrasing, sich etwas antun vs. sich umbringen). The judgment items have no partial credit in the exam, and they have none here either.

When you score 12/12, rehearse the risk conversation aloud end to end: Haben Sie schon einmal daran gedacht, sich etwas anzutun? … Haben Sie einen Plan? … Ich nehme das sehr ernst. Wir finden gemeinsam eine Lösung. Calm, direct, in order. That is psychiatric German, rehearsed.

Documentation: write it the way German charts do

What you say in the room must survive in the chart — and German psychiatric documentation has its own conventions. Suizidalität verneint (denies suicidality) or Suizidgedanken bejaht, kein konkreter Plan (suicidal thoughts affirmed, no concrete plan) are the standard formulations; examiners often ask you to dictate the note after the scenario. Write affektarm (flat affect), antriebsgemindert (reduced drive), formale Denkstörung (formal thought disorder) as single compound adjectives — German psychiatry loves them. And document the safety plan you agreed: Krisenplan besprochen, Krisendienst-Nummer mitgegeben, Nachsorgetermin vereinbart. The candidate whose documentation mirrors their conversation passes twice: once in the room, once on paper.

Frequently asked questions

Should I say Suizid or Selbstmord in German?

Always Suizid. Selbstmord (self-murder) carries moral condemnation; Freitod romanticises it. Suizid is the neutral clinical term examiners expect.

How do I ask a patient about self-harm in German?

Haben Sie schon einmal daran gedacht, sich etwas anzutun? Sich etwas antun is the standard gentle phrasing — never sich umbringen with a patient. Follow up directly: Haben Sie einen Plan?

What is person-first language in German psychiatry?

Der Mensch mit Schizophrenie, not der Schizophrene. Never use a diagnosis as a label for a person — examiners score person-first phrasing as professional communication.

How do I say panic attack and mood swing correctly?

Die Panikattacke — note Attacke, not Anfall, in psychiatric context. Die Stimmungsschwankung is the neutral term; never call a patient launisch (moody).

What should I say right after a patient discloses suicidal thoughts?

Ich nehme das sehr ernst — validate before moving on. Then build the alliance: Sie sind nicht allein damit and Wir finden gemeinsam eine Lösung, always with a concrete next step.

Which treatment terms matter most for the exam?

Die Verhaltenstherapie (CBT, often VT in notes), das Antidepressivum (plural Antidepressiva — effect takes weeks, say so), die Krisenintervention (name the local Krisendienst), die stationäre Behandlung, and die Nachsorge — arranged before discharge.
