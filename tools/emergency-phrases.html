    When seconds count, German must come first

    Emergency communication is the one corner of medical German where hesitation is not an option. In the Notaufnahme (emergency department) or at the bedside of a deteriorating patient, nobody waits while you conjugate. The examiner knows this, and the FSP scenario that drops you into an emergency tests one thing above all: can you produce the right sentence instantly, in the Sie-form, without translating through English first? This tool gives you 10 emergency frames — the 112 call, patient commands, and assessment questions — with swappable slots, plus a builder and a 12-round quiz that punishes the one-slot errors that cost time.

    The frames follow the order a real emergency follows. First the call: Hier ist ein Notfall! Kommen Sie bitte sofort hierher. The German emergency call is famously structured — the dispatcher wants Wer, Wo, Was, Wie viele, Warten (who is calling, where, what happened, how many injured, wait for questions) — and the number is 112, Europe-wide. Note sofort hierher: in German emergency language you name the urgency and the destination in one breath. The slot options give you the four destinations you will actually name: sofort hierher, in die Notaufnahme, zum Unfallort, in Zimmer 4.

    The handover sentence: consciousness and breathing

    The first clinical sentence of any emergency is the handover state: Der Patient ist {state}. Er atmet {resp}. These two frames cover the ABCDE opening — airway and breathing plus the disability letter. Bewusstlos (unconscious) vs. bei Bewusstsein (conscious) vs. nicht ansprechbar (unresponsive) are three different states with three different clinical meanings; mixing them up in the handover misleads the team. Er atmet nicht / flach / normal / unregelmäßig — four breathing states, one word each, drilled until they come out without thought.

    Why drill this? Because the examiners test exactly this sentence pair in the shock-room scenario, and because in real life the team acts on what you say. The quiz distractor sets force the distinction: Der Patient ist bewusstlos vs. nicht ansprechbar differ by one slot — and in the exam, one slot is the whole difference between pass and fail on that item.

    Commands: short imperatives, Sie-form, no jargon

    Emergency commands to the patient are imperatives in the Sie-form — short, unambiguous, repeated if needed. Bleiben Sie bitte ruhig liegen. Atmen Sie bitte ruhig weiter. Bewegen Sie den Kopf nicht. Three properties every command needs: the correct pronoun (Sie, never du — professionalism under pressure is still professionalism), one verb of action, and a politeness particle (bitte) that keeps the frightened patient cooperating. Note Bewegen Sie sich nicht — the reflexive command for "do not move" — vs. the targeted Bewegen Sie den Kopf nicht; the exam tests whether you can command the body part, not just the body.

    Atmen Sie bitte ruhig weiter deserves special attention: it is the single most-spoken sentence in any respiratory emergency, and its three slot options (ruhig weiter, tief ein und aus, nicht so schnell) map to the three breathing problems you coach through. Drill it until it is muscle memory — patients in respiratory distress cannot parse complex sentences, and neither can a candidate who has not rehearsed.

    Assessment questions: the four that buy time

    While the team assembles, you assess — and the four assessment frames are your German toolkit: Haben Sie {sym}? (do you have…), Seit wann haben Sie {sym}? (since when…), Nehmen Sie regelmäßig {med}? (do you regularly take…), and the therapeutic promise Wir geben Ihnen jetzt {tx}, das hilft gegen {sym}. The symptom slot covers the four red flags that recur in every emergency scenario: Schmerzen in der Brust (chest pain), Atemnot (breathlessness), Schwindel (dizziness), Übelkeit (nausea).

    Seit wann — since when — is the onset question every emergency history needs, and learners systematically underuse it; the frame puts it first-class. Nehmen Sie regelmäßig Blutverdünner? is the medication question with the highest stakes in an emergency (bleeding risk, thrombolysis decisions), which is why Blutverdünner sits among the slot options alongside Insulin and Blutdrucktabletten. And Wir geben Ihnen jetzt Sauerstoff, das hilft gegen Atemnot is the reassurance frame: name what you are doing and what it helps against — patients tolerate procedures they understand.

    How to drill: the 60-second round

    Build a full emergency script: the 112 call, the handover pair, two commands, one assessment question, the therapeutic promise. Copy it and deliver it aloud in under 60 seconds — that is the time budget a real shock-room handover gets. Then swap every slot and run it again: different patient, different state, different symptoms. The frames stay; the emergencies change.

    Drill the whole chain daily until the sequence is automatic — call, handover, commands, assessment, treatment promise — because in a real emergency the order is fixed and the words must be too. Then take the quiz: every distractor differs by exactly one slot — bewusstlos vs. nicht ansprechbar, flach vs. unregelmäßig, ruhig weiter vs. tief ein und aus — so it tests the one-slot precision emergencies demand. When you score 12/12, run the script from memory at full speed: that is emergency German, rehearsed.

    Frequently asked questions

    What number do I call for an emergency in Germany?

    112 — Europe-wide. The German dispatcher expects the five Ws: Wer, Wo, Was, Wie viele, Warten (who is calling, where, what happened, how many are injured, wait for questions before hanging up).

    How do I hand over a patient's state in German?

    Der Patient ist bewusstlos / bei Bewusstsein / nicht ansprechbar / verletzt — then the breathing state: Er atmet nicht / flach / normal / unregelmäßig. These two sentences are the ABCDE opening of every emergency handover.

    How do I give a command to a patient in German?

    Use short Sie-form imperatives: Bleiben Sie bitte ruhig liegen. Atmen Sie bitte ruhig weiter. Bewegen Sie den Kopf nicht. Keep the bitte particle — a frightened patient cooperates better with a polite command.

    What is the difference between bewusstlos and nicht ansprechbar?

    Bewusstlos means unconscious (loss of consciousness); nicht ansprechbar means unresponsive (cannot be roused to respond). They are different clinical states — the handover must use the right one.

    Which assessment questions matter most in an emergency?

    Haben Sie [symptom]? (current symptom), Seit wann haben Sie [symptom]? (onset), Nehmen Sie regelmäßig [medication]? (regular drugs, especially blood thinners). Onset and medication questions are the ones learners most often forget.

    How do I reassure a patient while treating them?

    Name what you are doing and what it helps against: Wir geben Ihnen jetzt Sauerstoff, das hilft gegen Atemnot. Patients tolerate procedures they understand.
