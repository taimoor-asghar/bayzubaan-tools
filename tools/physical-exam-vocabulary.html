The skill nobody practises: narrating the examination

Most German learners preparing for the Fachsprachenprüfung drill the anamnesis until it is fluent — and then go silent during the physical examination. The simulated patient lies there while the candidate palpates mutely, announces nothing, and asks Tut das weh? as if it were the only sentence that exists. The examiners notice. In the FSP's second part, the patient reports on communication, and "the doctor explained each step" is one of the things being scored. This tool teaches the 36 sentences that fill that silence: what a German doctor says, moment by moment, while examining.

The phrases are organised by body region because examinations are organised by body region: head and neck, chest, abdomen, extremities, neurological, skin. Six phrases per region cover the arc of a focused exam — announce the step, give the instruction, ask the key question. Ich höre jetzt Ihr Herz und Ihre Lunge ab before the stethoscope touches skin. Bitte halten Sie jetzt die Luft an for the heart sounds. Tut das Klopfen weh? after percussion. Learn them as sequences, not sentences, and the examination narrates itself.

Ankündigen: announce everything

The master rule of the German examination room is ankündigen — announce. Ich taste jetzt Ihre Lymphknoten ab. Ich klopfe jetzt Ihren Brustkorb ab. Ich prüfe jetzt Ihre Reflexe. The pattern is always ich + present tense + jetzt: I am doing this now. Patients must never be surprised by your hands, your hammer, or your cold stethoscope. Non-native candidates tend to examine first and explain after, if at all — usually because the announcement sentence did not come to mind in time. The fix is drilling these until Ich werde… / Ich … jetzt is a reflex triggered by reaching for the instrument.

Notice the verbs: abtasten (palpate), abklopfen (percuss), abhören (auscultate) — all separable, all with the prefix at the end, all standard clinical German. Ich höre jetzt Ihr Herz und Ihre Lunge ab is the single most-used examination sentence in German medicine; if you learn one phrase from this tool, learn that one. And note Bescheid sagen in the abdominal announcement — sagen Sie Bescheid, wenn es weh tut (let me know if it hurts) — the polite imperative wrapped in a conditional, which is how German doctors give instructions that involve pain.

Instructions: the imperative with bitte

Examination instructions are imperatives, and in the Sie form they are polite by construction — but bitte is what makes them kind. Bitte öffnen Sie den Mund weit. Bitte ziehen Sie die Knie an. Bitte entspannen Sie das Bein ganz. Compare the bare versions and feel the difference: the examination room is full of small vulnerabilities — undressing, lying down, being touched — and bitte is the linguistic acknowledgment of each one. The FSP examiners, playing the patient, register every missing bitte as a small coldness.

Two instructions deserve special attention. Bitte legen Sie sich hin und entspannen Sie den Bauch — relaxation is half the abdominal examination, and tense muscles hide everything; saying it explicitly is clinical, not just polite. Folgen Sie bitte meinem Finger mit den Augen shows the dative at work (meinem Finger) inside a sentence you will say a hundred times in neurology. And Berühren Sie mit dem Zeigefinger Ihre Nasenspitze — the finger-nose test — is worth memorising word-perfect because it is long, and long sentences are where candidates stumble mid-exam.

The questions inside the examination

Three question types recur during any examination. Pain checks: Tut es hier weh? Und hier? — short, rhythmic, asked constantly during palpation; Tut das Klopfen weh? after percussion. Comparisons: Sind beide Beine gleich warm? — the extremity exam is bilateral, and naming the comparison teaches the habit. Sensory checks: Spüren Sie das? Ist es spitz oder stumpf? — the cotton-swab-and-pin routine, with the forced choice (spitz oder stumpf) that gets a usable answer from a vague patient.

Note Juckt der Ausschlag? — jucken (to itch) is the verb learners never drill and patients use constantly. And Seit wann haben Sie diesen Ausschlag? ties the onset question to the visible finding — the examination and the history are one conversation, not two. The skin section's Haben Sie neue Cremes oder Waschmittel benutzt? teaches the contact-allergy history at the moment of inspection, which is when it belongs; candidates who save it for "later" usually forget it.

How to drill: the silent-patient method

Pick one region. Read its six phrases in order, aloud, with the pauses where the patient would comply — Bitte öffnen Sie den Mund weit (pause) Sagen Sie bitte „Aaa“. Mime the examination as you speak: reach for the imaginary stethoscope when you say Ich höre jetzt Ihr Herz und Ihre Lunge ab. The gestures anchor the sentences; in the exam, the gesture will pull the sentence out of you. Do all six regions this way, twice.

Then take the quiz: it runs the clinical direction — situation to phrase — with distractors from neighbouring regions, so Ich höre jetzt Ihre Darmgeräusche ab competes against Ich höre jetzt Ihr Herz und Ihre Lunge ab, the exact confusion pair. The correct answer is always the phrase for that exact moment: percussion announced before tapping, breath-holding for the heart, relaxation before palpation. When you score 12/12, narrate a full head-to-toe examination from memory: that is the FSP's second part, rehearsed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between abtasten, abklopfen and abhören?

Abtasten is palpation (feeling with hands), abklopfen is percussion (tapping), abhören is auscultation (listening). All three are separable verbs used constantly in the German examination — and all three should be announced before you do them.

Why must I announce each examination step?

Patients must never be surprised by your hands or instruments. In the Fachsprachenprüfung the simulated patient reports on communication, and explaining each step (ankündigen) is explicitly scored.

Should instructions to patients always include bitte?

Yes — bitte turns a clinical imperative into kind care. Bitte öffnen Sie den Mund weit sounds like a doctor; Öffnen Sie den Mund! sounds like an order. Examiners register every missing bitte.

How do I ask about pain during palpation?

Short and rhythmic: Tut es hier weh? Und hier? — asked constantly while your hands move. For percussion specifically: Tut das Klopfen weh? Always ask; never assume silence means no pain.

What does Bescheid sagen mean in the abdominal exam?

Bescheid sagen means to let someone know — sagen Sie Bescheid, wenn es weh tut (tell me if it hurts) is the standard pain warning before abdominal palpation, more natural than the literal translation.

Is narrating the exam really tested in the FSP?

Yes — the second part of the Fachsprachenprüfung is a physical examination with a simulated patient who reports on your communication. Silent examining is one of the most common reasons candidates lose marks there.
