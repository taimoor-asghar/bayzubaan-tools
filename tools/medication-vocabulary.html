    Why dosage German is its own language

    Medication instructions look simple — Nehmen Sie eine Tablette zweimal täglich — until you realise that every word is doing precise work and there is no room for approximation. Zweimal täglich is not morgens und abends: the first means roughly every twelve hours, the second names clock times. Vor dem Essen is not nüchtern: the first means thirty minutes before, the second means on a completely empty stomach. Patients act on these distinctions, and the FSP examiners test whether you can produce them exactly. This tool gives you 10 dosage frames with swappable slots — form, amount, frequency, timing, duration — plus the 16 abbreviations that cover every German prescription, and a quiz that punishes imprecision the way real dosing does.

    The builder works the way discharge counselling works: pick the form (Tablette, Kapsel, Tropfen, Saft, Salbe), the amount, the frequency, the timing, the duration — and watch the complete instruction compose itself. Nehmen Sie eine Tablette zweimal täglich. Bei Bedarf nehmen Sie eine Tablette, höchstens vier pro Tag. Tragen Sie die Salbe zweimal täglich auf die betroffene Stelle auf. Each frame is a sentence a German doctor actually says at discharge, and the slots let you generate the hundreds of variants you will need across patients. Add the ones you fumble to your script and drill them aloud: dosage instructions are spoken, not just written, and they must come out fluently while the patient is already reaching for their bag.

    The frequency system: täglich, 1-0-1, and the clock

    German dosing has three frequency languages and you must speak all three. Words: einmal/zweimal/dreimal täglich — the spoken standard. Notation: 1-0-1 — the written standard, morning-noon-evening, universal on prescriptions and medication plans. Clock times: morgens und abends — used when the timing matters relative to the day rather than the interval. The quiz tests the boundaries between them: zweimal täglich vs. morgens und abends are different instructions, and picking the wrong one is a real error, not a style choice.

    The abbreviation table is the Rosetta Stone: b.B. (bei Bedarf — if needed), tgl. (täglich), p.c./a.c. (after/before meals, from the Latin every prescription still uses), p.o. (by mouth), s.c./i.m./i.v. (the three injection routes). Note max. — höchstens vier pro Tag is the safety ceiling that must accompany every bei Bedarf instruction; a PRN order without a maximum is an incomplete order, and examiners mark it as one.

    Timing relative to food: the four-way distinction

    Vor dem Essen, nach dem Essen, zum Essen, nüchtern — four timings, four different pharmacological meanings. Nüchtern (on an empty stomach) is the strictest: nothing for hours before. Vor dem Essen means about thirty minutes before; zum Essen means with the meal; nach dem Essen means after. Patients routinely collapse these into "with food", which is why the instruction must be explicit and why the frame Nehmen Sie die Tablette {timing} exists as a drill: the slot forces you to choose, and choosing is the clinical act.

    Two more frames carry safety weight. Setzen Sie das Medikament nicht plötzlich ab — abrupt discontinuation is dangerous for beta-blockers, steroids, antidepressants and more; eigenmächtig (on your own) and ohne Rücksprache (without consulting us) are the softer variants for less critical drugs. Die Behandlung dauert zwei Wochen — stating the duration prevents the two classic patient errors: stopping when feeling better, and continuing forever. And bis die Packung aufgebraucht ist (until the pack is finished) is the natural endpoint for antibiotics, worth memorising whole.

    Forms and their verbs

    Each dosage form has its verb: tablets and capsules are genommen (taken), ointment is aufgetragen (applied), syrup is genommen but measured in millilitres. Tragen Sie die Salbe zweimal täglich auf die betroffene Stelle auf shows the separable auftragen splitting around the whole instruction — Tragen Sie … auf — which is why learners mangle it; the frame lets you drill it until the split is automatic. Nehmen Sie 5 ml Saft zweimal täglich teaches the millilitre dosing patients get wrong most often — always name the measuring device in real counselling (mit dem Messbecher), even though the frame keeps it short.

    Storage is part of the instruction too: Bewahren Sie das Medikament kühl und trocken auf, im Kühlschrank, außerhalb der Reichweite von Kindern — the last one is legally expected for every household with children. These sound like afterthoughts; in the FSP they are scored as completeness. The candidate who counsels storage counsels like a German doctor.

    How to drill: the discharge round

    Build a five-instruction discharge script: one scheduled tablet, one PRN tablet with maximum, one ointment, one syrup, one duration sentence. Copy it and deliver it aloud as if the patient is leaving — briskly, clearly, checking understanding after each (Haben Sie noch Fragen dazu?). Change the slots each round: different drug, different timing, different maximum. The frames stay; the prescriptions change.

    Then take the quiz: every distractor differs from the answer by exactly one slot — eine vs. zwei, Tablette vs. Kapsel, zweimal vs. dreimal — so it tests the precision dosing requires. The two abbreviation questions test the written register. When you score 12/12, counsel a full medication plan from memory: that is the discharge conversation, rehearsed.

    Frequently asked questions

    What does 1-0-1 mean on a German prescription?

    Morning–noon–evening dosing: 1-0-1 means one tablet in the morning, none at midday, one in the evening. 1-0-0 is once daily in the morning; 0-0-1 once in the evening. This notation is universal in German prescribing.

    What is the difference between zweimal täglich and morgens und abends?

    Zweimal täglich means twice daily at roughly 12-hour intervals; morgens und abends names clock times (morning and evening). They are different instructions — use the one that matches the drug's required interval.

    What do p.c. and a.c. mean?

    Latin abbreviations still used on German prescriptions: p.c. (post cibum) = after meals, a.c. (ante cibum) = before meals. P.o. means by mouth, s.c./i.m./i.v. are the injection routes.

    What is the difference between nüchtern and vor dem Essen?

    Nüchtern means on a completely empty stomach (nothing for hours); vor dem Essen means about 30 minutes before a meal. They are different instructions with different pharmacological reasons — never collapse them.

    Why must a bei Bedarf instruction always include a maximum?

    Because a PRN (as-needed) order without a ceiling is incomplete and unsafe: Bei Bedarf nehmen Sie eine Tablette, höchstens vier pro Tag. Examiners mark a missing maximum as a real error.

    How do I tell a patient not to stop a drug abruptly in German?

    Setzen Sie das Medikament nicht plötzlich ab — for critical drugs. Softer variants: nicht eigenmächtig (not on your own) or nicht ohne Rücksprache (not without consulting us) for less critical ones.
