    Why phrases beat words in the clinic

    Knowing die Schmerzen does not make you able to take a pain history. Clinical communication runs on whole phrases — fixed, polite, complete sentences that you produce without assembling them word by word. Seit wann haben Sie die Schmerzen? is not four words plus grammar; it is one chunk, retrieved whole, the way native doctors retrieve it. This tool gives you 40 of those chunks across the five situations that structure every clinical encounter: the first visit, the pain history, the physical examination, the informed-consent discussion, and discharge.

    The dialogue builder is the heart of the tool. Reading phrases is passive; assembling a consultation from them is active. Add the phrases in the order you would actually use them — greeting, chief complaint, onset, history, examination announcements — then copy the script and say it aloud, start to finish, as if a patient were sitting in front of you. That rehearsal is the closest thing to the Fachsprachenprüfung's conversation part that you can do alone, and five minutes of it daily will do more for your fluency than an hour of silent reading.

    The Sie rule and the politeness grammar

    Every phrase here uses the Sie form — in German healthcare you address adult patients with Sie, always, no exceptions. The FSP examiners will mark you down for a single du slipped into a patient conversation. Notice how the imperative softens with bitte: Bitte legen Sie sich hin, Atmen Sie bitte tief ein und aus. A bare imperative (Legen Sie sich hin!) sounds like an order; with bitte it sounds like care. That one word is the difference between a doctor and a drill sergeant.

    Also note the modal verbs doing politeness work: Ich möchte Ihnen den Eingriff erklären (möchte, not will), Sie müssen nüchtern bleiben (müssen is fine here — it is a medical instruction, not a request). And the subjunctive-free indirectness of Wäre es möglich… constructions, which you can add once these 40 are solid. Politeness in German clinical speech is grammatical, not just lexical — it lives in the verb forms.

    Scenario 1 & 2: the anamnesis

    The first visit and the pain history are the FSP's first exam part, and they follow a fixed arc: greeting → chief complaint → onset → character → history → social history. The eight Erstgespräch phrases walk that arc exactly. Two deserve emphasis: Seit wann bestehen die Beschwerden? uses bestehen (to have existed) rather than haben — the formal register examiners expect — and Was machen Sie beruflich? opens the occupational history more naturally than the blunt Sind Sie berufstätig?

    The Schmerzanamnese set is the highest-yield eight phrases in the whole tool. Pain is the most common presenting complaint, and its history has a canonical structure (onset, location, character, radiation, relieving/aggravating factors, associated symptoms, night pain). Memorise these eight as a block and you can take any pain history in German. The 1-to-10 scale question and the radiation question (Strahlen die Schmerzen aus?) are the two that most directly drive clinical decisions — get them word-perfect.

    Scenario 3: narrating the examination

    The examination phrases teach the most under-practised clinical skill: narrating what you are doing while you do it. Ich werde Sie jetzt untersuchen, Ich höre jetzt Ihr Herz und Ihre Lunge ab — the patient must never be surprised by your hands or your stethoscope. In the FSP, the simulated patient reports on exactly this: did the doctor explain each step? The phrases are short because they are spoken mid-action; learn them so they come out while your attention is on your hands, not on your grammar.

    Tut das weh? deserves a note: it is asked constantly during palpation, and the natural rhythm is ask–press–ask–press. Non-native speakers tend to either forget it entirely or ask it in English word order. Drill it as a reflex, not a sentence. And Sie können sich wieder anziehen closes the examination with dignity — never skip the closing, in the exam or in real life.

    Scenarios 4 & 5: consent and discharge

    The Aufklärung phrases carry legal weight: informed consent in Germany requires the doctor to explain the procedure, its risks, and alternatives, and to confirm understanding (Haben Sie noch Fragen?) before the signature. The sequence matters — explanation, risks, questions, then Bitte unterschreiben Sie hier. Reversing that order is not just rude, it is legally fragile. Sie müssen nüchtern bleiben is the instruction patients get wrong most often, so say it slowly and check understanding.

    Discharge is where safety-netting lives: Bei Fieber oder starken Schmerzen kommen Sie sofort wieder is the single most important discharge sentence, because it tells the patient exactly when to return. Pair it with the follow-up appointment (Kommen Sie in einer Woche zur Kontrolle) and the medication instruction (Nehmen Sie die Tabletten wie verordnet), and you have a complete, safe discharge in three sentences. Close with Gute Besserung! — warmth is clinical too.

    How to practise: the full-consultation drill

    Build a complete script: 3–4 phrases from Erstgespräch, all 8 from Schmerzanamnese, 4–5 from Untersuchung, 3 from Aufklärung, 4 from Entlassung. Copy it, print it or put it on your phone, and deliver it aloud to an imaginary patient — including the pauses where the patient would answer. Time yourself: a fluent delivery of 20 phrases takes about three minutes. When you can do it without reading, you are ready for the FSP conversation part at the phrase level.

    Then take the quiz: it tests English-to-German production, the harder direction and the one the exam actually requires. The distractors come from the same scenario, so Was lindert die Schmerzen? competes against Was verstärkt die Schmerzen? — the exact confusion pair that trips learners up. Every wrong answer shows the usage note, so even the quiz is teaching. When you score 10/10 twice in a row, rebuild the script from memory: that is the final test, and the one that mirrors the exam.

    Frequently asked questions

    Should I use du or Sie with patients?

    Always Sie with adult patients in German healthcare — no exceptions. A single du in the Fachsprachenprüfung's patient conversation costs marks. Every phrase in this tool uses the Sie form.

    What is the difference between Beschwerden and Symptome?

    Beschwerden is what the patient reports (complaints); Symptome is the more technical term. With patients, say Beschwerden — it is the neutral clinical word examiners expect in the anamnesis.

    Why does the Aufklärung sequence matter?

    Informed consent in Germany legally requires: explanation of the procedure, disclosure of risks, opportunity for questions, then signature. Asking Haben Sie noch Fragen? before Bitte unterschreiben Sie hier is not politeness — it is the law.

    How is this different from the medical flashcards tool?

    The flashcards teach individual terms (die Anamnese, der Befund); this tool teaches complete clinical sentences and how to sequence them into a consultation. Terms are vocabulary — phrases are performance.

    What does nüchtern bleiben mean exactly?

    To remain fasting — nil by mouth, usually from midnight before a procedure. It is the pre-op instruction patients misunderstand most often, so say it slowly and confirm they understood.

    How many phrases do I need for the FSP conversation?

    There is no fixed number, but 40 well-drilled phrases covering greeting, history, examination, consent, and discharge handle the vast majority of the conversation part. Fluency with these beats shaky knowledge of 200.
