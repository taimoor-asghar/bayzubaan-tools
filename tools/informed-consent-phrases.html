    The Aufklärungsgespräch: the heart of the Fachsprachenprüfung

    No part of the medical German exam matters more than the informed-consent conversation — das Aufklärungsgespräch. In Germany, informed consent is not a courtesy; it is a legal duty rooted in the patient's right to self-determination (Selbstbestimmungsrecht). A valid consent rests on three pillars, and examiners score whether your German delivers all three: what is planned (the procedure), what could go wrong (the risks), and what else could be done (the alternatives). Miss one pillar and the consent — and your exam performance — is incomplete. This tool gives you 10 frames that cover the full arc of the conversation: opening the Aufklärung, naming procedure, risks and alternatives, the aftercare, the questions, the agreement, the signature, the right of withdrawal, and the safety net.

    The frames mirror how the conversation actually flows in a German clinic. You open with Ich muss Sie über {proc} aufklären — note muss: the Aufklärung is an obligation, not a choice, and saying so signals to the examiner that you know your duty. Wir planen {proc} anchors the planned measure. Then the two pillars examiners listen for hardest: Die häufigsten Risiken sind {risks} and Eine Alternative wäre {alt}. Get these two sentences fluent and you carry half the marks of the whole scenario; fumble them and nothing else saves you.

    Risks: the three tiers every doctor must name

    Risk Aufklärung is tiered. Common risks (Blutungen und Infektionen — bleeding and infections) must always be named. Serious risks — even if rare — must be named when they are typical for the procedure: Verletzung benachbarter Organe (injury to neighbouring organs), eine erneute Operation (a repeat operation). Specific risks belong to the intervention itself: Thrombosen after surgery, allergische Reaktionen with contrast or blood products. The frame Die häufigsten Risiken sind {risks} with its five slot options covers the territory most FSP scenarios probe.

    Language matters as much as content. Risks must be named in words the patient can actually understand — Thrombose needs the gloss Blutgerinnsel, Infektion needs no jargon around it. Examiners reward the candidate who says Blutungen rather than hiding behind hämorrhagische Komplikationen. The quiz distractor sets force this discipline: they differ by exactly one slot, so precision — Blutungen und Infektionen vs. Thrombosen — is what scores.

    Alternatives: the pillar candidates forget

    Alternatives are the Aufklärung pillar most candidates drop under pressure — and dropping it costs the consent its validity. Every consent conversation must name what else could be done, including doing nothing: Eine Alternative wäre abzuwarten und zu beobachten (wait-and-see), eine medikamentöse Behandlung (drug treatment), eine Physiotherapie (physiotherapy), keine Behandlung (no treatment), ein anderer operativer Zugang (a different surgical approach). Note that keine Behandlung is itself a valid alternative the law requires you to mention.

    The aftercare frame — Nach dem Eingriff müssen Sie {after} — closes the loop between consent and the patient's real life: sich zwei Wochen schonen (take it easy for two weeks), bis morgen früh nüchtern bleiben (fast until tomorrow morning), täglich zur Wundkontrolle kommen (come daily for wound checks), Blutverdünner spritzen (inject blood thinners). A consent that ignores the aftermath is a half-consent; examiners notice.

    The closing ritual: questions, agreement, signature, withdrawal

    The last four frames are the choreography of a lawful consent. Haben Sie Fragen zu {topic}? — always ask, and name the topics: dem Ablauf, den Risiken, den Alternativen, der Narkose. Sind Sie mit {proc} einverstanden? — the explicit agreement question, never assumed. Bitte unterschreiben Sie hier, wenn Sie mit {proc} einverstanden sind — the signature ties agreement to the written record, and documenting that the conversation happened (Aufklärung dokumentiert) is part of your legal duty.

    And the sentence most foreign doctors never knew existed: Sie können Ihre Einwilligung jederzeit widerrufen — you can withdraw your consent at any time, even after signing, even right before the procedure (bis unmittelbar vor dem Eingriff). This is the patient's core protection and a sentence examiners expect to hear. The final frame — Bei {symptom} melden Sie sich bitte sofort wieder — is the safety net: red-flag symptoms (starken Schmerzen, Fieber über 38,5 Grad, Blutungen aus der Wunde, Atemnot) with an unambiguous instruction to return.

    Language craft: Sie-form, softeners, plain words

    Every frame uses the Sie-form with zero du/dich/dir leakage — check this habit now, because an exam slip into du is scored as a professionalism error. Risks and alternatives land more humanely with softeners: würde instead of wird (Eine Alternative wäre…), könnte instead of wird for rare risks (In seltenen Fällen könnte…). And translate the jargon down: Aufklärung is informing, not a technical ceremony. Say the words a real patient would understand, and the examiner hears a doctor, not a textbook.

    How to drill: the consent round

    Build a full consent script for one procedure: open with Ich muss Sie über eine Magenspiegelung aufklären, walk through Wir planen…, Die häufigsten Risiken sind…, Eine Alternative wäre…, Nach dem Eingriff müssen Sie…, then the closing ritual. Copy it and deliver it aloud, steadily, with a pause after Haben Sie Fragen zu den Risiken? — the pause is where real consent happens. Change the procedure and slots each round; the frames stay, the patients change.

    Then take the quiz: every distractor differs by exactly one slot — wrong procedure, wrong risk, wrong alternative, wrong aftercare — so it tests the one-slot precision that lawful consent requires. When you score 12/12, run the full script from memory for a second procedure: that is the Aufklärungsgespräch, rehearsed.

    Frequently asked questions

    What are the three pillars of German informed consent?

    What is planned (the procedure), what could go wrong (the risks), and what else could be done (the alternatives). All three must be communicated for the consent to be valid — examiners score whether your German delivers all three.

    How do I open an Aufklärungsgespräch in German?

    Ich muss Sie über [procedure] aufklären. The muss signals that informing the patient is your legal duty, not a courtesy — examiners listen for this professional framing.

    How do I tell a patient they can withdraw consent?

    Sie können Ihre Einwilligung jederzeit widerrufen — even after signing, even right before the procedure (bis unmittelbar vor dem Eingriff). This is the patient's core legal protection.

    Must I mention doing nothing as an alternative?

    Yes. Keine Behandlung (no treatment) is a valid alternative that German law requires you to name, alongside drug treatment, physiotherapy, wait-and-see, or a different surgical approach.

    Which risks must I name in the risk Aufklärung?

    Always name common risks (bleeding, infections), serious typical risks even if rare (injury to neighbouring organs, repeat operation), and procedure-specific risks (thrombosis, allergic reactions) — in plain language the patient understands.

    How do I phrase the aftercare and safety net in German?

    Nach dem Eingriff müssen Sie… covers the aftercare (schonen, nüchtern bleiben, Wundkontrolle, Blutverdünner). The safety net is: Bei [symptom] melden Sie sich bitte sofort wieder — with red-flag symptoms named explicitly.
