    The third FSP part, in writing

    The Fachsprachenprüfung has three parts: the patient conversation, the examination, and the documentation — the Arztbrief. Candidates obsess over the first two and wing the third, which is backwards: the documentation part is the most predictable of the three, because German discharge letters follow a fixed structure with fixed phrasing. Aufnahme, Anamnese, Befund, Diagnose, Therapie, Procedere — six sections, each with its canonical sentence frames. This tool gives you 20 of those frames with swappable slots, a draft builder that assembles a letter skeleton, and a quiz that tests whether you can tell Arztbrief German from spoken German.

    The single most important thing to understand: written clinical German is a different register from spoken clinical German. You say to the patient Seit wann haben Sie die Schmerzen?; you write Anamnestisch besteht seit drei Tagen: retrosternale Schmerzen. You say Ich höre jetzt Ihr Herz ab; you write Auskultatorisch unauffällig. The spoken language is full sentences with Sie; the written language is nominalised, passive, abbreviated, and Sie-free. Mixing the registers — writing Der Patient hat mir erzählt, dass… — is the classic documentation error, and it is instantly recognisable to examiners.

    The six sections and their frames

    Aufnahme establishes the facts: Die stationäre Aufnahme erfolgte bei akuten Bauchschmerzen — note erfolgte (took place), the passive-adjacent verb that opens half of all German letters, and the art of admission (als Notfall, elektiv, über die Notaufnahme). Anamnese compresses the history into noun phrases: Anamnestisch besteht seit drei Tagen: Belastungsdyspnoe — the adverb anamnestisch does the work of a whole spoken paragraph. Vorerkrankungen: arterielle Hypertonie and Zustand nach Herzinfarkt 2021 show the telegraphic list style; keine bekannt closes a list cleanly and must never be omitted — an undocumented negative is an unasked question.

    Befund is where the examination becomes text. Klinischer Untersuchungsbefund: unauffällig — unauffällig (unremarkable) is the most-written word in German medicine, and using it correctly marks you as an insider. The abbreviations are mandatory: RR for Blutdruck, HF for Herzfrequenz. Laborchemisch erhöhtes CRP von 80 mg/l shows the adverb-opening pattern; V. a. Appendizitis shows Verdacht auf abbreviated, the standard hedge for unconfirmed findings. Diagnose is one line — Diagnose: akute Appendizitis — plus the differential: Differentialdiagnostisch wurde eine Cholezystitis erwogen, which documents reasoning, not just conclusions, and examiners specifically look for it.

    Therapie and Procedere: the action sections

    Therapie uses erfolgte again — Therapeutisch erfolgte eine laparoskopische Appendektomie — the same verb as the admission, now for treatment. Die Operation verlief komplikationslos is the sentence surgeons most like to write; regelrecht (as expected, unremarkable course) is its quieter sibling. The postoperative paragraph is structured by the adverb postoperativ and the ordinal day: Wundinfekt am 3. postoperativen Tag, antibiotisch behandelt — complication, timing, and management in one compressed line. This compression is the skill: the spoken version would take four sentences.

    Procedere is the plan, and it is where the letter earns its keep for the GP. Entlassung in die Häuslichkeit — discharge home — is the classic close. Kontrolle beim Hausarzt in einer Woche assigns the follow-up explicitly. Safety-netting goes in writing too: Bei Fieber über 38,5 °C ist eine sofortige Wiedervorstellung erforderlich — erforderlich (required) gives it legal weight. Arbeitsunfähigkeit besteht für zwei Wochen addresses the sick note, which every German discharge letter must. And the letter closes with thanks — Wir danken für die Zuweisung — using Zuweisung (referral for admission) vs. Überweisung (referral for further care) vs. Mitbehandlung (co-management) correctly, a distinction examiners love to test.

    The dosing notation and other micro-conventions

    Metoprolol 50 mg 1-0-1 — the 1-0-1 notation means morning-noon-evening doses, and it is universal in German documentation. ASS 100 mg 1-0-0 is once daily in the morning. Writing morgens und abends instead is not wrong, but it marks you as outside the register. Similarly: decimal commas (38,5 °C), µl for microlitres, mg/l for CRP. These micro-conventions are invisible when right and glaring when wrong — the FSP documentation is graded by native readers who notice.

    The abbreviations deserve a deliberate study pass: RR, HF, V. a., Z. n. (Zustand nach), AU (Arbeitsunfähigkeit), ASS (acetylsalicylic acid). Each appears in the frames here in its natural habitat, which is how abbreviations should be learned — in sentences, not lists. And note what is never abbreviated in a letter: the diagnosis line, the procedure name, and the thank-you. Formality has its own hierarchy.

    How to drill: the skeleton method

    Build a complete letter skeleton: one frame from Aufnahme, three from Anamnese, three from Befund, two from Diagnose, two from Therapie, three from Procedere. Copy the draft — you now have a 14-sentence Arztbrief skeleton in correct written German. Then rewrite it by hand with different slots: different diagnosis, different findings, different plan. The frames stay; the cases change. After five different skeletons, the register is yours.

    Then take the quiz: it tests documentation judgment — which sentence belongs in the letter — with distractors from neighbouring sections, so Laborchemisch unauffällig competes against Klinischer Untersuchungsbefund: unauffällig, the exact section-confusion the exam tests. When you score 12/12, write a full letter from a case vignette without looking at the tool: that is the FSP's third part, rehearsed.

    Frequently asked questions

    What are the six sections of a German Arztbrief?

    Aufnahme (admission), Anamnese (history), Befund (findings), Diagnose (diagnosis), Therapie (treatment), Procedere (plan). Every German discharge letter follows this structure — learn the frames per section.

    What is the difference between spoken and written clinical German?

    Spoken German uses full sentences with Sie (Seit wann haben Sie die Schmerzen?); written German is nominalised, passive and Sie-free (Anamnestisch bestehen retrosternale Schmerzen seit drei Tagen). Mixing the registers is the classic documentation error.

    What does V. a. mean in a German medical letter?

    V. a. abbreviates Verdacht auf — suspicion of. V. a. Appendizitis means suspected appendicitis: the standard hedge for unconfirmed findings in German documentation.

    How is medication dosing written in German letters?

    With the 1-0-1 notation for morning-noon-evening: Metoprolol 50 mg 1-0-1 means morning and evening. ASS 100 mg 1-0-0 is once daily in the morning. This notation is universal in German documentation.

    What is the difference between Zuweisung, Überweisung and Mitbehandlung?

    Zuweisung is a referral for admission, Überweisung a referral for further/outpatient care, Mitbehandlung is co-management. The letter's thank-you line (Wir danken für die Zuweisung) must use the right one.

    Is the documentation part really tested in the Fachsprachenprüfung?

    Yes — the third part of the FSP is writing an Arztbrief from a case. It is the most predictable part because the structure and phrasing are fixed, which makes it the highest-yield part to prepare with frames like these.
