Dictation — typing exactly what you hear — is one of the oldest and most effective exercises in language learning, and German is the perfect language for it. German spelling is far more regular than English: once you train your ear to hear the difference between "den" and "dem", between a long vowel and a short one, between "ß" and "ss", you can write almost anything you hear. But the reverse is also true: if you cannot hear those differences, your spoken German will always sound approximate. This dictation tool closes that loop: listen, type, get graded word by word.

How dictation trains three skills at once

First, listening: real speech runs words together, swallows endings, and drops the pitch at the end of statements. A dictation forces you to catch every article, every verb ending, every preposition — the small words that carry the grammar. Second, spelling: German capitalises all nouns, doubles consonants after short vowels ("kommen", "Wasser"), and uses ä, ö, ü where English would write plain vowels. You only learn these by producing them. Third, grammar: when you mishear "dem Mann" as "den Mann", the grader shows you exactly which case ending you missed, turning an ear problem into a grammar lesson. The tool's word-level feedback makes this visible after every sentence.

How to use this trainer

Start at A1 even if you are not a beginner. Pick a sentence, press play, and type exactly what you hear. You can replay the sentence as many times as you like — in the early rounds, slow playback (the turtle button) helps you catch the endings. After checking, study the words marked red: read the correct sentence out loud, then dictate the same sentence to yourself again. Move up to B1 when your A1 scores are consistently above 90%, and to B2 when B1 feels easy. The Challenge mode mixes all levels with no translation shown — the closest thing to a real German exam dictation.

How grading works

Each sentence is graded word by word, case-insensitively, ignoring punctuation: capitalisation and commas do not affect your score, because the point of the exercise is what you heard. A fully correct word earns a full point; a completely wrong or missing word earns nothing. The most common error pattern is dropped endings — "der" heard as "die", "-en" heard as "-e". The feedback panel lists every word you missed with the correct form, so you can see whether your errors are spelling errors, ear errors, or grammar errors. Track your average per level with the progress bars; a 90% average means you are ready to move up.

Typical German dictation traps

Five things catch almost every learner. 1. Noun capitalisation: in your typed answer it does not affect the grade, but in real writing every noun takes a capital — train yourself to notice which words are nouns as you listen. 2. ß vs ss: "Straße" (street) has a long vowel, "Fluss" (river) a short one; the spelling tells you the vowel length if you know the rule. 3. Silent h (Dehnungs-h): "gehen", "sehen", "fahren" — you cannot hear the h, you must know it is there. 4. ie vs i: "sie" (she/they) vs "sie" is fine, but "Biene" vs "Binne" is not — the ie is a long i:. 5. Word-final devoicing: "Tag" sounds like "Tak", "lieb" like "liep"; write the g and b anyway. The B2 sentences above are chosen to contain all five traps.

What your score is actually telling you

A score is only useful if you know how to read it. Below 60%, the problem is usually vocabulary: you cannot type words you have never met, so the right move is more reading and word-bank work before more dictation. Between 60% and 85%, the errors shift to the ear — missed articles, swallowed endings, confused prepositions. This is the sweet spot where dictation helps the most, because every red word marks a sound your brain is filtering out. Above 90% on the first listen, your ear is genuinely ahead of your level and you should move up; hovering at 95% on A1 for weeks teaches you nothing new. One pattern to watch for: if your misses are always the same kind of word (articles, say, or verb prefixes), that is a grammar signal, not an ear signal — review the case drills or separable-verb quizzes for that specific topic, then return to dictation and watch the score jump.

Frequently asked questions

What is a dictation exercise?

You listen to a German sentence and type exactly what you hear. It trains listening, spelling, and grammar at once: you must catch every article, ending, and preposition. This tool plays sentences via your device's German voice and grades your answer word by word.

Why is my dictation score low even though I understood the sentence?

Understanding is easier than reproducing. Most low scores come from missed endings (dem vs den, -en vs -e) and dropped small words — exactly the sounds learners filter out. The word-level feedback shows you precisely which words your ear is skipping.

Does capitalisation affect my grade?

No. Grading is case-insensitive and ignores punctuation, so 'der hund' scores the same as 'Der Hund'. But you should still train yourself to capitalise nouns — in real German writing it is mandatory.

Should I start at A1 or B2?

Start at A1 regardless of your level and move up when your average passes 90%. A1 sentences drill the core patterns (articles, verb endings, capitalisation) that every higher-level error is built on.

How does the Challenge mode work?

Challenge mode mixes all 24 sentences across A1–B2 and hides the English translation, so you cannot use it as a crutch. It is the closest simulation of a real exam dictation in this trainer.
