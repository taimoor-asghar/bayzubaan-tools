Komposita: German's Lego-brick words

German builds nouns the way children build with Lego: snap simple words together and you get a new, precise word. Krank (sick) + Haus (house) = Krankenhaus (hospital). Hand + Schuh (shoe) = Handschuh (glove — a "hand-shoe"). Fern (far) + Seher (seer) = Fernseher (television — a "far-seer"). Once you can split compounds, tens of thousands of "unknown" German words become transparent, because most long German words are just short ones in a trench coat. The head-last rule: the key to every compound

Every German compound has a head: the LAST part. The head decides two things — the grammatical gender and the core meaning. Der Handschuh is masculine because der Schuh is masculine; it is fundamentally a kind of shoe (for the hand). Die Kaffeemaschine is feminine because die Maschine is; it is fundamentally a machine. Das Haustier is neuter because das Tier is; it is fundamentally an animal. Everything before the head just modifies it. So when you meet an unknown compound, read it backwards: find the head first, get the category and gender, then work left to narrow it down. …versicherung → some kind of insurance; Kranken… → sick-related; Krankenversicherung → health insurance. Two seconds, no dictionary.

Fugenlaute: the glue between the bricks

Often the parts do not join cleanly — a linking sound, the Fugenlaut, sits between them. The splitter highlights these in blue. The common ones: -s- (Arbeit-s-zeit, Liebling-s-essen, Geburt-s-tag — often after feminine nouns ending in -heit/-keit/-ung/-schaft, and in many fixed compounds), -n- (Woche-n-ende, Treppe-n-haus — often after feminine nouns ending in -e), -en- (krank-en-Haus — often after weak masculine nouns and plurals), -e- (Hund-e-hütte), -er- (Kind-er-garten — often after plural -er forms). There is no fully reliable rule for which Fugenlaut a compound takes; native speakers just know. For learners, the practical move is recognition, not production: when splitting a word, strip a candidate Fugenlaut and see if both sides become real words — exactly what the splitter's algorithm does.

The famous monsters

German compounds have no theoretical length limit, which produces legendary words. The classic: Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaftskapitän (42 letters) — Donau (Danube) + Dampf (steam) + Schiff (ship) + Fahrt (journey) + s + Gesellschaft (company) + s + Kapitän (captain): the Danube steamship company captain. Try it in the splitter — it handles all seven parts. The official record-holders were legal terms like Rindfleischetikettierungsüberwachungsaufgabenübertragungs­gesetz (63 letters, a beef-labelling law, repealed 2013 partly because of its name). These are party tricks, but they prove the point: length is not complexity. Every monster is just familiar bricks.

Splitting strategy for unknown words

When you meet a long unknown word in a text: 1. Find the head (last part) — category and gender secured. 2. Peel off parts from the right, stripping Fugenlaute, until the remainder is a word you know. 3. Translate literally, then adjust to the idiomatic meaning — the literal gloss is a scaffold, not the answer (Handschuh is a glove, not footwear). 4. Watch for traps: some compounds are not compositional. Der Kummerspeck ("grief-bacon") means weight gained from emotional eating — no bacon involved. Die Schadenfreude is compositional ("harm-joy") but lexicalised. And separable-prefix verbs (anfangen) look like compounds but follow verb rules instead. When in doubt, split first and check a dictionary second — the split tells you what to look up.

Compounds in medical German: where this pays off fastest

Nowhere does compound-splitting pay off faster than in medical German — the Fachsprachenprüfung runs on compounds. Die Krankenversicherung (health insurance), die Krankmeldung (sick note), die Überweisung is not a compound but der Überweisungsschein is (referral slip). Die Blutdruckmessung (blood pressure measurement) = Blut + Druck + Messung; die Herzfrequenz is a loanword, but die Herzinsuffizienz splits into Herz + Insuffizienz. Der Behandlungsplan (treatment plan) is masculine because der Plan is; der Entlassungsbrief (discharge letter) because der Brief is. In the Anamnese you will hear Vor-erkrankungen (pre-existing conditions), Begleiterkrankungen (comorbidities), Schmerzmittel (painkillers = Schmerz + Mittel). Splitting these live, mid-conversation, is a genuine clinical skill: patients describe symptoms in compounds (Bauchschmerzen, Kopfschmerzen, Rückenschmerzen — all transparent once you split), and documentation demands the precise full form. A learner who can split knows the system that generates thousands: every -haus is a kind of house, every Kinder- is child-related, every -maschine is a machine. Frequently asked questions

How do you split a German compound word?

Read it backwards: the LAST part (the head) sets the gender and core meaning. Then peel off parts right-to-left, stripping linking sounds (Fugenlaute) like -s-, -n-, -en- until each piece is a word you know. This tool's splitter automates it.

What is a Fugenlaut in German?

A linking sound between compound parts: -s- (Arbeitszeit), -n- (Wochenende), -en- (Krankenhaus), -er- (Kindergarten). There is no fully reliable rule for which one appears — recognise and strip them when splitting rather than trying to predict them.

Which part of a German compound decides the gender?

Always the last part (the head). Der Handschuh is masculine because der Schuh is masculine; die Kaffeemaschine is feminine because die Maschine is.

What is the longest German word?

Famous record-holders include Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaftskapitän (42 letters, Danube steamship company captain) and the 63-letter Rindfleischetikettierungsüberwachungsaufgabenübertragungsgesetz (a repealed beef-labelling law).

What does Handschuh literally mean?

Hand-shoe — a glove. German compounds are often delightfully literal: Kühlschrank (cool-cupboard) = fridge, Fernseher (far-seer) = TV, Staubsauger (dust-sucker) = vacuum cleaner.

Are all long German words compounds?

Mostly, but watch for traps: separable-prefix verbs (anfangen) follow verb rules, some compounds are not compositional (Kummerspeck = grief-bacon, i.e. emotional-eating weight), and loanwords can just be long. Split first, verify second.
