Why one number beats five guesses

Learners usually know their skills unevenly: "my reading is okay, my speaking is terrible, grammar… no idea." Vague self-knowledge produces vague study plans. The overall level estimator compresses all five skills into one 10-minute sitting and returns two things: a combined CEFR snapshot (the level your German actually functions at, averaged across skills) and per-skill bars (which skill is pulling the average down). The second output is the valuable one. A B1 snapshot with A2 listening and B2 reading is a completely different study plan from a flat B1 — and most learners are spikier than they think.

How the 20 questions are built

Four questions per skill, arranged easy → hard within each skill, so the test probes the full A1–C1 range. Reading (1–2): case government in context (trotz + genitive) and recognising the passive infinitive — the two structures that separate intermediate readers from advanced ones. Vocabulary (3–5, 19): false friends (Kaution), reflexive verb frames (sich bewerben um), and the B2 connector allerdings. Grammar (6–12, 20): the greatest-hits ladder — question words, reflexive accusative, relative pronouns, Konjunktiv II, Konjunktiv I, genitive prepositions, comparatives, strong verbs. Listening (13–15): reduced forms (haste), spoken idioms, announcement vocabulary — the ear-skills written tests miss. Pragmatics (16–18): formal register, restaurant German, polite requests — the social software. Twenty questions can't certify a level, but they triangulate it surprisingly well: each skill's 4 questions act as an independent mini-probe.

Reading your snapshot

The headline band is your functional average — roughly the level of material and conversation you can handle across the board. Then read the skill bars (the result screen ranks missed topics by skill). The golden rule: your weakest skill caps your real-world level. B2 grammar with A2 listening means you are A2 in any conversation — the ear is the bottleneck, and an hour of listening practice beats an hour of grammar at that point. Common patterns: reading leads, listening lags (the classic — fix with the listening readiness quiz and the wpm ladder); grammar leads, pragmatics lags (textbook learners — fix with the etiquette and small-talk tools); everything flat (take the placement test for your band and climb normally). Re-take monthly: the headline should creep up, but watch for a skill jumping a full colour — that's the real victory.

Snapshot vs placement tests: use both

This estimator is a wide, shallow probe — 4 questions per skill. The placement tests (tools 56–60) are narrow, deep probes — 25 questions on one level's grammar and vocabulary. Use the estimator first: it tells you which placement test to take and which skill needs work. Then take that level's placement test for the deep diagnosis. Example: estimator says B1 with weak grammar → take the B1 placement test, which pinpoints the exact grammar topics. Estimator every month (the weather report), placement test after each study block (the medical check-up). Together they replace every "what level am I?" anxiety with data.

The skill-balancing study method

Once you know your weakest skill, spend half your study time on it until it stops being the weakest. This feels wrong — working on strengths is more fun — but the weakest skill has the highest marginal return: moving listening from A2 to B1 transforms your real-world German more than moving reading from B1 to B2. Rotate in 2-week blocks: weeks 1–2: weakest skill gets 50% of time; weeks 3–4: re-test, re-balance. Concrete pairings: weak listening → slow news + shadowing + the listening quiz; weak speaking → daily aloud narration + Tandem + the speaking quiz; weak writing → 200 words/week + the writing checklist; weak grammar → the specific drill tools the placement test names; weak vocabulary → top-1000/2000 with spaced repetition. Balanced profiles climb fastest because no single bottleneck wastes the other skills' potential.

What the snapshot can't tell you

Four questions per skill is a probe, not an exam — a lucky guess moves a skill bar. Treat close calls (e.g. 10 vs 11, the A2/B1 border) as "borderline" and confirm with the placement test. The estimator also can't measure production under pressure: recognising würde in a multiple-choice question is not the same as producing it mid-sentence at speed. That's what the writing checklist and speaking quiz are for — they test what you can do, while this tests what you know. The honest level is the lowest of the three. And none of these replace a real exam when it counts (visa, university, job) — they tell you when you're ready to book one, which is exactly their job.

Your monthly measurement ritual

First Sunday of the month, 30 minutes: 1. this estimator (the snapshot), 2. your current level's placement test (the deep check), 3. the writing checklist (production honesty). Log three numbers: snapshot band, placement score, writing level. Three months of rising numbers is proof your method works; three months flat means the method needs changing, not your effort — usually it means too much passive study and too little production. Learners who measure monthly improve roughly twice as fast as learners who don't, not because measuring teaches, but because it forces the re-balancing that measuring reveals. The tools are the instruments; you're still the musician.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from the placement tests?

This estimator is wide and shallow: 20 questions across all five skills giving a combined snapshot. The placement tests (A1–C1) are narrow and deep: 25 questions on one level. Use the estimator monthly to find your band and weakest skill, then the placement test for a deep diagnosis of that level.

My skills are very uneven. Which level am I?

Your weakest skill caps your real-world level: B2 grammar with A2 listening means you function at A2 in conversation. The snapshot shows the average, but the skill bars show the truth — spend half your study time on the weakest skill until it stops being the weakest.

How accurate is a 20-question estimate?

It's a triangulation, not an exam — treat borderline scores as borderline and confirm with the placement test. It's designed to answer 'which level/test/skill next?', which it does reliably, not to certify a level.

Should I trust this over a real exam?

No. These tools tell you when you're ready to book a real exam (Goethe, telc, ÖSD) — they don't replace one. When the estimator, the placement test, and the writing/speaking checks all agree on a level, that's a strong signal you're exam-ready.

How often should I take the estimator?

Monthly, as part of a 30-minute ritual: estimator + your level's placement test + the writing checklist. Log the three numbers — three months of rising numbers proves your method works; three months flat means change the method.

My listening is much weaker than my reading. Is that normal?

Completely — it's the most common pattern. Reading is recognition at your own pace; listening is recognition at 160 wpm with reduced forms. Fix it with the listening readiness quiz, the wpm speed ladder, and shadowing — an hour of listening beats an hour of grammar when the ear is the bottleneck.
