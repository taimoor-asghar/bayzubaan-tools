Wohnungssuche: Germany's most competitive sport

Finding a flat in Germany — the Wohnungssuche — is famously brutal in the big cities. In Berlin, Munich or Hamburg, a good listing can draw hundreds of applications within hours, and viewings (Besichtigungen) feel like casting calls. The language barrier makes it harder: listings are dense with abbreviations, agents reply fastest to German messages, and the Mietvertrag (lease) is a legal document you are expected to understand. These 40 phrases cover the whole hunt — answering listings, surviving viewings, decoding the lease, checking the fittings, and introducing yourself to the neighbours — each with a mini-dialogue so you hear how the exchange actually sounds.

The first skill is reading the Anzeige (listing). German ads run on abbreviations: ZKBB (Zimmer, Küche, Bad, Balkon), EBK (Einbauküche — fitted kitchen), KM (Kaltmiete — cold rent, without utilities), WM (Warmmiete — warm rent, including heating), NK (Nebenkosten — utilities), KT (Kaution — deposit), WG (Wohngemeinschaft — shared flat), OG/DG (Ober-/Dachgeschoss — upper/attic floor). The two rents matter enormously: the Kaltmiete is the headline figure, but the Warmmiete is what leaves your account. Always ask 'Wie hoch sind die Nebenkosten?' — utilities of 150–250 euros a month on top of the cold rent are normal, and the difference between ads is often hidden there.

The viewing: questions that win flats

At the Besichtigung, you are being evaluated as much as the flat — landlords choose tenants, not the other way round. Arrive on time (see the etiquette quiz), dress neatly, and bring the Unterlagen (documents) agents expect: the Selbstauskunft (self-disclosure form), proof of income (last three payslips), Mietschuldenfreiheitsbescheinigung (certificate of no rent arrears from your previous landlord), and a copy of your ID. Having these ready signals reliability more than any sentence can.

The questions in the Besichtigung tab do double duty: they extract information and they signal competence. 'Wie hell ist die Wohnung?' (south-facing windows are gold), 'Sind die Wände schallisoliert?' (thin walls ruin lives), 'Wer wohnt über/unter der Wohnung?' (a night-shift worker below you is a conflict waiting to happen), 'Wie wird geheizt?' (district heating Fernwärme is cheap; night-storage heaters Nachtspeicher are expensive). Ask 'Wann muss ich mich entscheiden?' to learn the timeline — and then decide fast, because the next viewer is already deciding.

The Mietvertrag: what you are actually signing

German tenancy law (Mietrecht) is strongly tenant-friendly, but the Mietvertrag still deserves a careful read. Key clauses: the Kaution (deposit) — legally capped at three months' Kaltmiete, payable in three instalments if you ask; the Kündigungsfrist (notice period) — three months to month-end for tenants, by law; Staffelmiete (graduated rent — fixed annual increases, legal) versus Indexmiete (inflation-linked); and the Kleinreparaturklausel (minor-repairs clause — you pay small fixes up to ~100 euros per incident, capped annually). Unbefristet (open-ended) is the standard you want; befristet (fixed-term) needs a legal reason from the landlord. Untervermietung (subletting) always needs written permission — 'Darf ich untervermieten?' is not a formality, it is a legal requirement.

The Wohnungsübergabe (handover) closes the deal: walk through with the landlord, note every scratch in the Übergabeprotokoll (handover protocol), photograph everything, and read the meters. This protocol is what stands between you and a deposit dispute years later. And note the very German detail that surprises newcomers: flats are routinely rented unmöbliert (unfurnished) — and 'unfurnished' often means without a kitchen. The Einbauküche (fitted kitchen) is frequently the previous tenant's property, taken over for an Abstand (compensation payment). 'Kann die Einbauküche übernommen werden?' is therefore one of the most financially important sentences in this whole tool.

After the keys: neighbours and the Hausordnung

Introduce yourself — 'Guten Tag, ich bin neu hier eingezogen' — on day one; Germans remember who didn't. Read the Hausordnung (house rules, posted in the stairwell): it governs quiet hours, stairwell cleaning rotas (Kehrwoche — the Swabian institution, now widespread), and rubbish duties. Learn the Müllabfuhr schedule immediately. Apologise in advance for moving noise ('Entschuldigung für den Lärm beim Umzug') — a small sentence that buys enormous goodwill. And register your address within 14 days (see the bureaucracy vocabulary tool): the Anmeldung is the administrative twin of the physical move.

Frequently asked questions

What do KM, WM and NK mean in German flat listings?

KM = Kaltmiete (cold rent, without utilities), WM = Warmmiete (warm rent, including heating and utilities), NK = Nebenkosten (utilities, ~150–250€/month). The Warmmiete is what actually leaves your account — always compare that figure.

How high can the deposit (Kaution) be in Germany?

Legally capped at three months' Kaltmiete (cold rent). You may pay it in three monthly instalments. It must be kept separate from the landlord's assets and returned after you move out, minus justified deductions.

What documents do I need for a flat viewing in Germany?

The standard set: Selbstauskunft (self-disclosure form), proof of income (last three payslips), Mietschuldenfreiheitsbescheinigung (no-rent-arrears certificate from your previous landlord), and ID. Having them ready signals reliability.

Do German flats come with a kitchen?

Often not — 'unmöbliert' (unfurnished) frequently means without a kitchen. The Einbauküche (fitted kitchen) usually belongs to the previous tenant and is taken over for an Abstand (compensation). Always ask: 'Kann die Einbauküche übernommen werden?'

What is the notice period for tenants in Germany?

Three months to the end of the month, by law — and this applies even if the contract says something longer. Fixed-term (befristet) contracts need a legal reason from the landlord; open-ended (unbefristet) is the standard.

Can I sublet my German flat?

Only with the landlord's written permission — Untervermietung without consent can be grounds for termination. Ask before you list, not after: 'Darf ich untervermieten?'
