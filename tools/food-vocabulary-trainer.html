Food vocabulary: the words you use three times a day

Food vocabulary is the highest-frequency vocabulary there is. You eat three times a day, you shop for groceries weekly, and every restaurant visit is a German exam you did not sign up for. This tool covers the 48 words that carry 90% of everyday food situations: fruit and vegetables, meat and fish, bread and dairy, drinks, spices and cooking verbs' nouns, and the restaurant script. Every entry ships with its article — because a German noun without its article is only half learned — plus a real example sentence you can steal and reuse.

The quiz runs in both directions for a reason. German→English trains recognition: reading a menu, understanding the waiter. English→German trains production: ordering, shopping, cooking with a German recipe. The production direction asks for the word with its article, because Ich nehme Apfel marks you as a beginner while Ich nehme den Apfel does not. Distractors come from the same food category, so der Pfeffer competes against das Salz and der Zucker — the exact confusion set your brain will face in a real kitchen.

Articles: the grammar hiding in the grocery bag

Food nouns are a tour of German gender with no reliable logic. Fruit is mostly feminine (die Banane, die Orange, die Erdbeere, die Traube) — except der Apfel. Vegetables lean feminine too (die Kartoffel, die Tomate, die Karotte, die Zwiebel) — except der Salat. Meat compounds are neuter because das Fleisch is neuter (das Rindfleisch, das Schweinefleisch, das Hähnchen), dairy splits three ways (die Butter, die Milch, die Sahne vs der Käse, der Joghurt), and drinks are mostly masculine (der Kaffee, der Tee, der Saft, der Wein) — except das Wasser, das Bier and die Limonade.

There is no shortcut around this: memorise each noun with its article as one unit. The browse view always shows them together (der Apfel — apple) so your eyes learn the pair. Say the article every time you drill — der, der Apfel; die, die Banane — until the pairing is automatic. This is the single habit that separates learners who "know the words" from learners who can actually use them in a sentence.

Shopping: the supermarket as a classroom

The German supermarket is free vocabulary practice. Sections are labelled: Obst & Gemüse (fruit & veg), Milchprodukte (dairy), Fleisch & Wurst (meat & sausage), Getränke (drinks), Gewürze (spices). Read every sign aloud. At the bakery counter (die Bäckerei), order by number and name: Zwei Brötchen und ein Vollkornbrot, bitte. At the cheese counter: 100 Gramm von dem Bergkäse, bitte. Quantities in German: ein Kilo Kartoffeln, eine Flasche Wasser, eine Packung Butter, eine Dose Tomaten.

Two shopping realities. First, Pfand: bottles and cans carry a deposit (8–25 cents), returned at the Pfandautomat — keep your empties. Second, bags cost money and cashiers are fast: have your 1-Euro-Münze ready for the cart and pack at speed. None of this is in textbooks; all of it is daily Germany.

Cooking verbs: the other half of the kitchen

Food nouns get you the ingredients; cooking verbs get you the recipe. The essential set: kochen (cook/boil), backen (bake), braten (fry/roast), schneiden (cut), hacken (chop), rühren (stir), würzen (season), probieren / abschmecken (taste/season to taste), servieren (serve). German recipes are imperative-heavy: Zwiebeln glasig anbraten (fry the onions until translucent), mit Salz und Pfeffer würzen (season with salt and pepper), bei 180 Grad backen (bake at 180 degrees).

Note abschmecken — literally "taste off", meaning to adjust the seasoning: Mit Salz und Pfeffer abschmecken. It has no single English equivalent and appears in nearly every German recipe. And gar: Das Hähnchen ist noch nicht gar means it is not cooked through — gar as "done/cooked" is kitchen-only German, unrelated to ganz (whole).

Dining out: the full restaurant script

The German restaurant runs on a fixed script, and this tool's sixth category is that script in order: die Speisekarte → die Vorspeise → die Hauptspeise → der Nachtisch → die Rechnung → das Trinkgeld. Order with Ich hätte gern… or Ich nehme…; ask Was empfehlen Sie? for the waiter's pick; compliment with Es hat sehr gut geschmeckt. The bill never comes automatically — Die Rechnung, bitte is mandatory — and the tip is 5–10%, rounded, handed directly to the server with Stimmt so, never left on the table.

Meal culture notes: das Frühstück is bread, cold cuts, cheese, and Brötchen; das Mittagessen is the main hot meal of the day; das Abendbrot / Abendessen is traditionally cold — bread, Wurst, Käse. Kaffee und Kuchen (coffee and cake, around 15:00–16:00) is a genuine institution. Knowing these rhythms turns food vocabulary from a word list into a lived routine.

Frequently asked questions

Why do German food nouns have such unpredictable articles?

There is no reliable logic — fruit is mostly feminine (die Banane) except der Apfel, meat compounds are neuter (das Rindfleisch), drinks mostly masculine (der Kaffee) except das Wasser and das Bier. Memorise each noun with its article as one unit.

How do I order food politely in German?

Use Ich hätte gern… (I would like…) or Ich nehme… (I'll have…). Ich will is too blunt for adults. Always ask for the bill: Die Rechnung, bitte — it never comes automatically.

What does 'abschmecken' mean in German recipes?

To taste and adjust the seasoning — Mit Salz und Pfeffer abschmecken. It appears in nearly every German recipe and has no single English equivalent.

What is Pfand in German supermarkets?

A deposit (8–25 cents) on bottles and cans, returned at the Pfandautomat machine. Keep your empties and feed them to the machine before checkout.

What are the main German meals of the day?

Das Frühstück (bread, cold cuts, Brötchen), das Mittagessen (the main hot meal), and das Abendbrot (traditionally a cold evening meal of bread, Wurst, and Käse). Kaffee und Kuchen around 15:00 is an institution.

How much should I tip in a German restaurant?

Round up 5–10% and say Stimmt so (keep the change) when paying cash, or state the total when paying by card. Hand it directly to the server — never leave it on the table.
