Konnektoren: the glue of fluent German

German examiners have a simple test for fluency: count the Konnektoren — the linking words — and check their grammar. A text that runs und … und … und is A2. A text that moves with weil, obwohl, dennoch, infolgedessen is B2 and above. This tool teaches 40 connectors in five families — causal, concessive, temporal, adversative/additive, consecutive/conditional — each with its word-order pattern and a full example sentence pair. The pattern is part of the entry (weil — Verb ans Ende) because the connector and its word order are one inseparable unit: learn the word without the pattern and you have learned nothing.

Three word-order regimes govern every connector in German. Regime one: subordinating conjunctions (weil, obwohl, wenn, sodass, bevor) send the finite verb to the end of their clause — weil ich in Deutschland arbeiten möchte. Regime two: coordinating conjunctions (denn, aber — and only these two, plus und/oder) leave the verb in second position — denn ich möchte in Deutschland arbeiten. Regime three: conjunctive adverbs (deshalb, trotzdem, jedoch, außerdem, infolgedessen) occupy position one and trigger inversion — deshalb blieb ich zu Hause. The single most-tested distinction in German exams is weil versus denn: same meaning, different regime, and the quiz below probes it relentlessly.

Causal connectors: giving reasons

The causal family answers warum. Weil is the default — subordinating, verb last: Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Deutschland arbeiten möchte. Denn means the same but coordinates: Ich lerne Deutsch, denn ich möchte in Deutschland arbeiten — verb second, and no comma-driven verb movement. Learners who write denn ich in Deutschland arbeiten möchte have mixed the regimes; it is the classic B1 error. Da is weil for reasons the listener already knows — Da ich krank war, blieb ich zu Hause — and it prefers the front position, reasoning before result.

The adverbial causals — deshalb, daher, aus diesem Grund — start a new main clause with inversion: Ich war krank, deshalb blieb ich zu Hause. They are the essay writer's workhorses because they link two full sentences without a subordinate clause. Aufgrund takes the genitive — Aufgrund des schlechten Wetters — and belongs to formal written German; wegen + genitive is its spoken cousin. Dank (thanks to) takes dative or genitive and is always positive — Dank deiner Hilfe habe ich die Prüfung bestanden — never use it for negative causes.

Concessive connectors: the although-family

The concessive family is where German essays earn their marks, because conceding an objection before rebutting it is the dialectical move. Obwohl subordinates — Obwohl es regnete, gingen wir spazieren — while trotzdem and dennoch are adverbs with inversion: Es regnete, trotzdem gingen wir spazieren. The three are not interchangeable in register: dennoch is the formal sibling, the one examiners want to see. Wenngleich (even though, formal) and auch wenn (even if) complete the subordinating set; trotz + genitive — Trotz des Regens — compresses the whole concession into a noun phrase, the most elegant option when the reason is a thing rather than a clause.

Zwar … aber is the paired concession — Es ist zwar teuer, aber es lohnt sich — and it is a favourite exam trap because both halves must be present: a zwar without its aber is a broken promise. Ungeachtet + genitive (Ungeachtet aller Warnungen) is the formal register's trotz; use it once per essay, in the strongest concession, and the examiner notices.

Temporal connectors: als versus wenn

The temporal family runs on one great distinction: als for single completed past events — Als ich jung war, spielte ich Fußball — versus wenn for repeated or future events — Wenn ich Zeit habe, lese ich. English when covers both; German splits them, and mixing them is the most audible learner error in the whole connector system. Während (while), bevor (before), nachdem (after — with the past perfect in the subordinate clause: Nachdem er gegessen hatte), sobald (as soon as), bis (until) all subordinate with the verb at the end. Seitdem (since, temporal) mirrors English since's time-meaning — not to be confused with causal da.

Adversative, additive, consecutive, conditional

Aber coordinates (ich war krank — verb second), jedoch inverts (ich war jedoch krank); the meaning is identical, the register is not — jedoch is the essay word. The additive chain außerdem, zudem, ferner climbs in formality: außerdem is neutral, zudem formal, ferner very formal (use once). Einerseits … andererseits frames the balanced paragraph; im Gegensatz zu + dative contrasts with a noun; stattdessen (instead) inverts and replaces the whole previous action.

The consecutive pair: sodass subordinates the result — Es regnete stark, sodass das Spiel ausfiel — while infolgedessen inverts as the formal adverb. The conditional set: wenn (if), falls (in case — the weaker, more hypothetical wenn), sofern and vorausgesetzt, dass (provided that — the contract lawyer's wenn). Andernfalls and sonst (otherwise) close the system: Beeil dich, andernfalls verpassen wir den Zug.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between weil and denn?

Same meaning, different word order: weil is subordinating (verb to the end — weil ich arbeiten möchte), denn is coordinating (verb stays second — denn ich möchte arbeiten). Mixing them — denn ich arbeiten möchte — is the classic B1 error.

When do I use als and when wenn for 'when'?

Als for single completed past events: Als ich jung war … Wenn for repeated or future events: Wenn ich Zeit habe … English 'when' covers both; German splits them, and mixing them is the most audible connector error.

What is the difference between obwohl, trotzdem and dennoch?

Obwohl is a subordinating conjunction (verb last): Obwohl es regnete … Trotzdem and dennoch are adverbs with inversion: Es regnete, trotzdem gingen wir … Dennoch is the formal register's trotzdem — the one examiners want to see.

Which connectors trigger inversion in German?

The conjunctive adverbs: deshalb, daher, trotzdem, dennoch, jedoch, außerdem, zudem, ferner, stattdessen, infolgedessen, andernfalls. They occupy position one, so the verb comes second — deshalb blieb ich zu Hause.

How do I say 'provided that' in formal German?

Sofern alle zustimmen … or the explicit Vorausgesetzt, dass das Wetter hält … — both subordinating, verb last. Falls is the weaker alternative (in case), for hypothetical conditions.

What is the zwar … aber construction?

The paired concession: Es ist zwar teuer, aber es lohnt sich (admittedly expensive, but worth it). Both halves must be present — a zwar without its aber is a broken promise, and examiners mark exactly that.
