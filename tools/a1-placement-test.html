What A1 means (and what this test checks)

A1 (Beginner) is the first rung of the CEFR ladder: you can understand and use familiar everyday expressions, introduce yourself, and ask and answer simple questions — slowly, with help. Concretely, A1 German means: the articles (der/die/das), basic plurals, nominative and accusative (plus dative glimpses), present tense of regular and key irregular verbs, sein and haben, nicht/kein negation, basic word order, separable verbs, numbers, time, and ~500 words of vocabulary. This test's 25 questions sample all of it — each question is tagged with its topic, so your result doesn't just give a score, it gives a diagnosis: exactly which topics leaked points.

How the scoring works

25 questions, one point each, no timer, no trick questions. 0–8: Below A1 — start with foundations (articles, top-1000 words, present tense). 9–14: A1.1 — early beginner; cases and plurals next. 15–19: A1.2 — late beginner; polish dative, separable verbs, perfect tense. 20–22: A1 pass — you're ready for A2. 23–25: Above A1 — take the A2 test immediately. The 25 questions, decoded

Every question here is a diagnostic probe, not a trivia item. Questions 1–2 test article gender — the foundation everything else stands on. 3–4 test plural formation, including the umlaut pattern (Kind → Kinder) that trips most beginners. 5–7 walk the case ladder nominative → accusative → dative; missing all three means cases need rebuilding from zero, missing only dative means targeted practice. 8–10 check present-tense conjugation including the irregulars (ist, not *bin for er). 11–12 test the nicht/kein distinction — the single most-tested A1 grammar point in real exams. 13–14 check verb position and separable prefixes, the two word-order features that define German sentence structure. 15–16 cover time and numbers, the survival skills. 17–18 sample core vocabulary. 19 is a set phrase (Guten Morgen!) — phrase knowledge, not grammar. 20–21 test question words. 22 possessives, 23 modals (note the missing -e: ich kann), 24 the perfect participle (including the *gemachen trap), 25 the most important preposition of origin. Read your misses against this map — it tells you exactly which chapter to open.

A1 to A2: the bridge

Passing A1 (20+) unlocks A2, and the bridge has a clear shape. New grammar: Präteritum (past narrative tense), reflexive verbs, prepositions with fixed cases, comparatives and superlatives, adverbs of time/manner/place, and first glimpses of Konjunktiv II (würde + infinitive). New skills: past narration (telling what happened yesterday), future plans, comparisons, and simple subordinate clauses (weil, dass, wenn). Vocabulary: from ~500 to ~1000 words — the top-2000 quiz (tool 32) becomes your daily driver. The A2 test (tool 57) probes all of this; take it the week you pass A1, while the grammar is warm. And keep this A1 test bookmarked — retaking it monthly should show a stable 23+, and any dip means a foundation topic needs a refresher before it undermines your A2 work.

After the test: your study loop

A placement test is a compass, not a verdict. The loop: test → study weakest topics (2 weeks) → retest. Each band's study plan links the precise tools: article strugglers get the article trainer, case strugglers get the case drills, vocabulary strugglers get the top-1000 browser. Don't study everything — study the top 2 missed topics; fixing those moves the score fastest. And when you pass A1 (20+), don't linger: take the A2 placement test the same week while the momentum is hot. Levels are meant to be climbed, not camped on.

Why placement beats guessing

Most learners misjudge their level — usually upward ("I did German in school, so B1?") — then drown in material that's too hard, or stagnate in material that's too easy. A 10-minute placement test replaces months of wrong-level study. It also fights the two great motivation killers: the plateau illusion (retaking shows +4 points you couldn't feel) and topic blindness (you knew dative was weak; now you know it's the weakness). Take this test today, the A2 test when you pass, and re-take each level test monthly. Your scores are the most honest progress metric you have — more honest than streak counters, more motivating than vague "keep going."
