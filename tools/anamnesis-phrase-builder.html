    Why frames, not phrases

    A phrasebook gives you Seit wann haben Sie die Schmerzen? — useful exactly once, for pain. A frame gives you Seit wann haben Sie {symptom}? — and with it you can ask about pain, nausea, dizziness, breathlessness, itching, or any symptom you will ever meet. The frame is the grammar with a socket; the slot is the vocabulary you plug in. Learn one frame and you own fifty questions. Learn fifty phrases and you own fifty questions. This tool is built on that arithmetic: 25 frames across the 8 sections of a complete anamnesis, each with swappable slots, each producing a correct, natural, polite German question.

    The builder works the way you will work in the clinic. Choose the section you are in — opening, pain history, past history, medications, allergies, social history, review of systems, closing — then fill the slots: the symptom, the condition, the detail. Watch the sentence compose itself in real time, add it to your script, and move on. When the script reads like a real history-taking from greeting to Haben Sie noch Fragen an mich?, copy it and say it aloud. The Fachsprachenprüfung's first part is exactly this: a structured anamnesis, in order, with the right questions at the right moment.

    The arc of the anamnesis

    Every history follows the same arc, and the eight sections of this tool follow it too. Eröffnung opens with an open question — Was führt Sie zu mir? — because closed questions at the start make patients report what you ask about, not what troubles them. Schmerzanamnese is the largest section because pain is the most common presenting complaint and its history has the richest structure: onset, localisation, intensity, radiation, relieving and aggravating factors, character. Vorerkrankungen and Medikamente establish the baseline; Allergien is asked explicitly because patients rarely volunteer it; Sozialanamnese covers smoking, alcohol, occupation, and home situation — the context every treatment plan needs; Systemabfrage sweeps for what the patient forgot to mention; Abschluss closes with the two questions that must never be skipped.

    Notice the grammar doing clinical work. Seit wann (since when) opens every onset question. Bestehen Allergien gegen…? uses bestehen (to exist) — the formal register examiners expect instead of the learner's haben. Wurden Sie schon einmal operiert? is passive because the patient is the receiver of surgery, not the doer — a distinction that matters in German. Gab es in Ihrer Familie…? uses gab es (was there) rather than haben, because family history is something that happened, not something anyone owns.

    Slot grammar: the details that make frames work

    Slots are not plug-and-play in German — the inserted word must agree with the frame. Every slot option here was written into its frame, not pasted: die Schmerzen fits Seit wann haben Sie {symptom}? because the accusative plural is right; der Schwindel fits Wie stark ist {symptom}? because the singular verb ist matches a singular noun. This is the discipline frames demand: every combination must be a sentence a native doctor could say, which is why the slot lists here are curated per frame rather than shared globally.

    The social-history slots show a second technique: the optional follow-up. Rauchen Sie{detail}? with — wenn ja, wie viel teaches you the reflex that separates a box-ticking question from a clinical one. Anyone can ask Rauchen Sie?; the doctor asks the follow-up. Drill the frame with its follow-up until the follow-up feels like part of the question — because in the exam, it is.

    The questions patients answer badly

    Some frames exist because patients systematically misreport. Nehmen Sie regelmäßig Medikamente? — the word regelmäßig is doing real work: patients who take a tablet "sometimes" will say yes to Nehmen Sie Medikamente? and no to regelmäßig, and the distinction changes dosing decisions. Vertragen Sie die Medikamente gut? is gentler than Haben Sie Nebenwirkungen? — patients downplay side effects when asked directly, but vertragen (tolerate) invites an honest answer.

    Ist Ihnen etwas Ungewöhnliches aufgefallen? is the softest way to ask about alarming symptoms, and softness is strategic: Haben Sie Blut im Stuhl? asked cold can make patients defensive, while the aufgefallen (noticed) frame lets them report without feeling accused of hiding something. These are the phrasing instincts the FSP tests under the label "patient-centred communication" — and they are all grammatical choices, not personality traits. You can learn them the way you learned the dative.

    How to drill: the script method

    Build a full anamnesis script: 3 frames from Eröffnung, 6–8 from Schmerzanamnese (or Systemabfrage if the case has no pain), 3 from Vorerkrankungen, 2 from Medikamente, 2 from Allergien, 3 from Sozialanamnese, and the 2 closers. That is a 20-question history — copy it, and deliver it to an imaginary patient, leaving pauses for answers. Change the slots each time: different symptom, different condition, different follow-up. The frames stay; the cases change. After ten different scripts, the frames are reflexes.

    Then take the quiz: it runs the hard direction, English prompt to German question, with distractors from the same section — Was lindert die Schmerzen? against Was verstärkt die Schmerzen?, the exact confusion pair. Every round is one frame you must produce, not recognise. When you score 12/12, rebuild a script from memory without looking at the tool: the frames should come out in order, slots filled, Sie-form intact. That is the exam, rehearsed.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between Beschwerden and Symptome?

    Beschwerden is what the patient reports (their complaints); Symptome is the more technical term. In the anamnesis, say Beschwerden — it is the neutral clinical word examiners expect.

    Why does this tool use frames with slots instead of fixed phrases?

    One frame with swappable slots generates dozens of correct questions. Seit wann haben Sie {symptom}? covers pain, nausea, dizziness and more — learning the frame multiplies your vocabulary instead of just adding to it.

    Should I ask Rauchen Sie? so directly?

    Yes — asked directly and without judgement, it is the standard clinical phrasing. The key is the follow-up (wenn ja, wie viel?), which turns a yes/no question into clinically useful information.

    What does bestehen mean in Bestehen Allergien?

    Bestehen means to exist or persist — Bestehen Allergien? is the formal register for Do allergies exist? Examiners expect this formal phrasing rather than the learner's Haben Sie Allergien? in the allergy history.

    How do I close an anamnesis properly?

    With the two catch-all questions: Gibt es noch etwas, das ich wissen sollte? (anything else I should know) and Haben Sie noch Fragen an mich? (any questions for me). Patients often reveal the real concern at this point — never skip the closer.
