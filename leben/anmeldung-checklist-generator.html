What the Anmeldung is — and why it comes first

The Anmeldung is the mandatory registration of your residential address with the local registration authority (Bürgeramt, Einwohnermeldeamt or Kundenzentrum — the name varies by city). It applies to everyone who moves into a flat in Germany: Germans, EU citizens and newcomers from every other country alike. It is also required when you move within Germany (then called Ummeldung) — your old registration is cancelled automatically when you register the new address.

The Anmeldung is the key that unlocks everything else: without the Meldebescheinigung (the registration certificate you receive on the spot) you cannot open a bank account, your employer cannot run payroll properly, and your health insurer and the immigration office will ask for it. Do it first, do it on time, and do it with complete documents — one missing paper means rebooking, and in big cities the next free slot can be weeks away.

The 14-day rule (§17 BMG), explained

Under §17 (1) of the Federal Registration Act (Bundesmeldegesetz), you must register within 14 days of moving in. The clock starts on your actual move-in day — the date your landlord enters on the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung — not on the day you signed the lease. Day 1 is the move-in day itself.

Missing the deadline is an administrative offence (Ordnungswidrigkeit) and can be punished with a fine of up to €1,000. In practice, many offices are lenient if you can show you booked the earliest available appointment — which is exactly why you should book the appointment the day you sign the lease, before you even have the keys. Save the booking confirmation: it is your proof that you acted in time.

If someone else goes to the appointment for you, they need a written power of attorney (Vollmacht) plus their own ID. For a family move, one parent can register the whole household in a single appointment — you don't need separate slots for each family member.

Situation extras: students, workers, families, shared flats

The core checklist is identical for everyone — German law does not sort newcomers by nationality. Only your residence status adds a document (non-EU citizens show their visa or residence permit), and your living situation adds a few:

  • Students: nothing extra at the Bürgeramt — but get your Immatrikulationsbescheinigung (enrolment certificate) ready anyway; you will need it within days for a student bank account and your semester ticket.
  • Workers and the self-employed: nothing extra at the Bürgeramt either. Your employer (or your tax adviser) will want a copy of the Meldebescheinigung afterwards.
  • Families: bring the children's birth certificates (originals) and your marriage certificate if you register as a married couple.
  • Shared flat / sublet (WG): the main tenant (Hauptmieter) is legally your Wohnungsgeber — they fill in and sign your Wohnungsgeberbestätigung. Keep a copy of your sublet contract (Untermietvertrag) for your own records.

At the appointment — and what comes after

The appointment itself takes about 10–15 minutes: the clerk checks your documents, enters your data and hands you the Meldebescheinigung on the spot. Check the printout before you leave — a misspelled name on the certificate will follow you into every later process.

What to do with the Meldebescheinigung next: give a copy to your employer, take it to your bank appointment, and keep it for the immigration office (Ausländerbehörde) if you need a residence permit. Your tax ID (Steuer-ID) is issued automatically after registration and mailed to your registered address — you don't apply for it separately. And note: the broadcasting fee (Rundfunkbeitrag) office registers every new address automatically, so expect their letter within weeks.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I have to register my address in Germany?

14 days from your move-in day (§17 (1) BMG). The clock starts on the actual move-in day — the date on your Wohnungsgeberbestätigung — not the lease-signing day. Missing the deadline is an administrative offence punishable by a fine of up to €1,000, so book your appointment as soon as you sign the lease.

What documents do I need for the Anmeldung?

Three core documents: a valid passport or national ID (original), the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung signed by your landlord, and the completed Anmeldeformular. Families add children's birth certificates and a marriage certificate; non-EU citizens add their visa or residence permit.

Can my rental contract replace the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung?

No. The contract proves the tenancy; the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung proves the actual move-in — and only the latter is accepted at the registration office. Your landlord is legally obliged to issue it within 14 days of your move-in (§19 BMG).

I live in a shared flat (WG) or sublet — who signs my form?

The main tenant (Hauptmieter): in a sublet they are legally your Wohnungsgeber (§19 (1) BMG), so they fill in and sign the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung. Agree it with the landlord where possible, and keep your sublet contract for your records.

Do I need an appointment at the Bürgeramt?

Almost always yes — book online right after signing the lease; in big cities slots can be gone weeks ahead. Use the city's official portal or call 115. Cologne and Frankfurt also run walk-in hours on Mondays and Wednesdays.

What do I get after the Anmeldung, and what do I do with it?

You get the Meldebescheinigung (registration certificate) on the spot. You need it for your bank account, your employer, your health insurer, and residence-permit appointments. Your tax ID (Steuer-ID) is issued automatically and mailed to your registered address.
