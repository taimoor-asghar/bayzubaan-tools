Estimate your monthly budget

How this estimator works

Germany's cost of living is not one number — it is a housing market with six different price levels and a set of fixed costs that barely move between cities. This estimator separates the two. Rent is city-specific and sourced: WG-room medians come from the Moses Mendelssohn Institut's analysis of wg-gesucht.de listings (autumn 2026), and 1-room rents from InterNations' city data. Transport uses the official 2026 prices: the Deutschlandticket at €63/month and the Deutschlandsemesterticket at €34.80/month for students. Student health insurance uses TK's published 2026 student tariff (about €141–€146/month including long-term care insurance). The Rundfunkbeitrag is the statutory €18.36/month per household. Groceries, utilities and leisure are estimate ranges drawn from 2026 expat guides (entri.app, allaboutberlin.com, proforg.com) — they are marked est. in the results. Everything is framed as a planning estimate: prices move, and your actual spending will differ.

The six cities at a glance

Berlin (€650 WG room): still the cheapest million-city for renters, with the deepest WG market in the country. Groceries around €255/month for one person cooking at home. The trade-off is competition — finding that €650 room takes persistence.

Munich (€850 WG room): Germany's most expensive city by a clear margin. Numbeo's 2026 rent index puts Munich at roughly double Leipzig. A 1-room flat of your own runs about €1,070 outside the centre. Budget at least €1,450/month as a student in a WG — and treat the visa blocked amount (€11,904 for 2026) as a floor, not a plan.

Hamburg (€650 WG room): priced like Berlin for rooms, with strong wages in trade and media. Note the housing market is tight enough that tenant associations flagged extreme cases in 2026 — always check a listing against the Mietpreisbremse (rent cap) before signing.

Cologne (€640 WG room): the most affordable of the western million-cities, popular with students and media workers. A 1-room flat averages about €830 outside the centre.

Frankfurt (€700 WG room, est.): banking salaries pull rents up — a 1-room flat averages about €960 outside the centre. If your job is in finance, the higher pay usually covers the difference; if not, consider nearby cities with S-Bahn connections.

Leipzig (€525 WG room, est.): the budget champion. Rents run 30–40% below Berlin, groceries about €225/month, and the cultural life punches far above the price. The classic choice for students and anyone whose work is remote.

The fixed costs every newcomer meets

Four costs surprise almost everyone. Health insurance is mandatory — you cannot enrol at a university or start most jobs without proof. Students under 30 pay the reduced tariff (about €141–€146/month); employees pay roughly half of 14.6% of gross salary plus the fund's supplementary rate, with the employer covering the other half. The Rundfunkbeitrag (€18.36/month) funds public broadcasting and applies to every household, whether you watch or not — in a WG, only one person registers and flatmates normally split it, which is why the student preset shows €6. The rental deposit (Kaution) can be up to 3 months of cold rent, payable before you move in — budget for it separately, because this estimator covers monthly costs only. And transport: the €63 Deutschlandticket covers all regional public transport nationwide, which for most newcomers replaces a car entirely.

Reading the lifestyle presets

Student assumes a WG room, the €34.80 Semesterticket, the €144 student insurance tariff, a split Rundfunkbeitrag, and modest leisure spending (€80). Single professional uses the full €63 Deutschlandticket, an estimated €310/month statutory insurance share, and higher leisure spending (€150). Couple doubles transport and insurance, multiplies groceries by 1.8 (couples don't eat twice as much), and keeps one Rundfunkbeitrag per household. Families can start from the couple preset and add roughly €200–€300 per child for food and activities — a deliberately rough rule of thumb, marked as such.

What the estimator leaves out

Honest framing matters, so here are the gaps. One-time costs are excluded: the Kaution deposit, furniture (German flats often come without a kitchen or even light fixtures), the €75 visa fee, and document translations. Rent figures are medians of advertised listings — actual offers in central districts run higher, and furnished short-term flats (Wunderflats, HousingAnywhere) run much higher. Income tax, church tax, and pension contributions are not modelled; the professional preset shows the employee's insurance share only. And prices move: the Deutschlandticket rose from €49 to €58 to €63 within three years, and a Rundfunkbeitrag increase to €18.64 from 2027 has been recommended. Re-check the fixed figures yearly.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is this cost of living estimator?

It is a planning estimate, not a quote. Rent medians come from the Moses Mendelssohn Institut's wg-gesucht.de analysis and InterNations (2026); transport uses the official 2026 prices (€63 Deutschlandticket, €34.80 Semesterticket); student health insurance uses TK's published 2026 tariff (€141–€146/month); the Rundfunkbeitrag is the statutory €18.36/month. Groceries, utilities and leisure are estimate ranges. Prices move — treat every figure as a starting point.

Why is Munich so much more expensive than Leipzig?

Housing drives almost the entire gap. Numbeo's 2026 rent index puts Munich at roughly double Leipzig, and advertised WG-room medians are about €850 in Munich versus €450–€600 in Leipzig. Groceries, transport and insurance cost nearly the same everywhere — only rent changes the total dramatically.

Do I really have to pay the Rundfunkbeitrag as a newcomer?

Yes. Every household pays €18.36 per month for public broadcasting (ARD, ZDF, Deutschlandradio), whether you watch or not. In a shared flat only one person registers and pays — flatmates usually split it. After your Anmeldung, the Beitragsservice writes to you automatically. BAföG and Bürgergeld recipients can apply for exemption.

Is health insurance really mandatory in Germany?

Yes — no enrolment or most jobs without proof of insurance. Students under 30 pay a reduced tariff of about €141–€146/month with TK in 2026 (health + long-term care combined). Employees pay roughly half of 14.6% of gross salary plus the fund's supplementary rate, split with the employer.

Which costs surprise newcomers most?

Three stand out. First, the rental deposit (Kaution): up to 3 months of cold rent, due before you move in. Second, German flats often come without a kitchen or light fixtures, so furnishing costs hit in month one. Third, the Rundfunkbeitrag letter arrives a few weeks after registration and confuses everyone the first time.

How does the 2026 blocked-account (Sperrkonto) amount relate to this budget?

Non-EU students must prove about €11,904 for 2026 (around €992/month) in a blocked account for the visa. That is a visa requirement, not a budget — in Munich or Frankfurt real spending runs higher, while in Leipzig it can run lower.
