Every flat in Germany owes the Rundfunkbeitrag — €18.36 per month, even without a TV. But many people pay who don't have to: benefit recipients, BAföG students away from home, severely disabled people, and flatmates who are already covered. Answer the six questions below and get a plain-English verdict on whether you can skip it, reduce it to €6.12, or are simply already covered.

What the Rundfunkbeitrag is — and why everyone gets the letter

Since 2013 the Rundfunkbeitrag has been a flat per-flat contribution that funds ARD, ZDF and Deutschlandradio: their TV channels, radio stations, websites and media libraries. It replaced the old device-based fee, which is why "but I don't own a TV" no longer gets you out of anything. The legal basis is the Rundfunkbeitragsstaatsvertrag (RBStV), in force as state law in all 16 Länder.

Shortly after your Anmeldung, the residents' registration office reports your address to the ARD ZDF Deutschlandradio Beitragsservice (the famous Cologne address). You then receive a letter asking you to register an account — the fee is owed from the month you moved in. Registering costs nothing; the account then collects €18.36 a month.

The fee in 2026: €18.36 per Wohnung

The state prime ministers decided in December 2024 to keep the fee at €18.36 per month for 2025 and 2026. It is charged per flat, not per person — a WG of five pays the same as a single person. Payment rhythms: €55.08 quarterly, €110.16 half-yearly, or €220.32 for the full year paid in advance.

Note on 2027: the KEF (the commission that calculates the broadcasters' funding need) recommended a small rise to €18.64/month from 2027. That is a recommendation, not a decision: all 16 state prime ministers must agree and every state parliament must ratify it — and several heads of government have already announced they will not support it. Until that process completes, €18.36 applies.

Who can be fully exempt (§4 RBStV)

The law lists the exemption grounds explicitly, and they are granted only on application — never automatically. Full exemption is realistic if you receive:

  • Bürgergeld (Grundsicherung für Arbeitsuchende, SGB II)
  • Grundsicherung im Alter und bei Erwerbsminderung or Hilfe zum Lebensunterhalt (SGB XII)
  • BAföG, BAB or Ausbildungsgeld — but only if you do not live with your parents
  • Asylbewerberleistungen (asylum-seeker benefits)
  • Blindenhilfe, or you are deafblind, or hold a Sonderfürsorgeberechtigung (special welfare entitlement)

Note the traps: regular ALG I or plain Wohngeld alone do not qualify automatically, and a disability card by itself doesn't either — it must be the RF marker (below) or one of the cases above. A granted exemption also covers your spouse or registered partner and children under 25 in the same household.

The €6.12 reduction: disability marker RF

If your Schwerbehindertenausweis carries the Merkzeichen RF (reduced ability to use public broadcasting because of a visual or hearing impairment, or other health reasons), you can get the fee cut to one third: €6.12 per month — a saving of €146.88 a year. This needs a separate reduction application with a copy of your disability card.

The Härtefall route: income barely above welfare level

There is also a hardship exemption for people whose income sits only marginally above social-welfare level. After a Federal Constitutional Court ruling (1 BvR 665/10), the law must cover cases "comparable" to benefit recipients: in practice, if your available income after housing costs is less than about the €18.36 monthly fee above your recognised need, a hardship application is worth filing — with proof of income and expenses attached. Renters with a small pension plus Wohngeld are the classic case.

Already covered: the one-per-flat rule

Only one contribution is due per flat. So: if your partner, parent or a flatmate already has an account and pays, you need nothing. Students living with their parents are covered by the household's account — and BAföG recipients living at home don't qualify for the exemption anyway. Special case: married or registered couples can have one of their two homes exempted on application (§4a RBStV).

How to apply (and what happens next)

1Go to rundfunkbeitrag.de → "Befreiung/Ermäßigung beantragen", or write to the Beitragsservice by post.
2Attach a copy of your proof: the current benefit notice (Leistungsbescheid), disability card with RF, or medical certificate for deafblindness.
3Apply immediately when you receive a benefit notice — the exemption is granted in writing for the period stated in your benefit decision. Keep paying until you hold the confirmation.
4If your benefit started months ago and you paid in the meantime, those contributions can be refunded for the covered period — reports note backdating of up to three years is possible. Check the dates on your confirmation letter.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay the Rundfunkbeitrag even if I don't own a TV?

Yes. The fee is per flat (€18.36/month), due regardless of devices or viewing habits. The only exits are a §4 exemption, the €6.12 reduction with RF, or being covered by a flatmate's account.

I just arrived in Germany — when does the fee start?

After your Anmeldung the registration office reports your address to the Beitragsservice, which writes to you automatically. The fee is owed from your move-in month — one account per flat.

Which benefits qualify for full exemption?

Bürgergeld, Grundsicherung/Hilfe zum Lebensunterhalt, BAföG away from parents, asylum-seeker benefits, Blindenhilfe, deafblindness and Sonderfürsorgeberechtigung — always on application with a copy of your benefit notice.

We share a WG — does everyone have to pay?

No — one fee per flat. If a flatmate already pays, you're covered. One person's exemption doesn't create a second bill either.

My income is barely above welfare level — any chance?

Possibly via a Härtefall application: if your available income after housing costs is less than about €18.36 above your recognised need, the hardship route (BVerfG 1 BvR 665/10) is worth trying with proof attached.

Is the fee really rising to €18.64 in 2027?

Only a KEF recommendation so far — all 16 state governments must agree and every parliament must ratify, and several have already refused. Nothing is decided.
