What SCHUFA is — and why every newcomer meets it

SCHUFA (Schutzgemeinschaft für allgemeine Kreditsicherung) is Germany's largest private credit bureau, headquartered in Wiesbaden. It holds data on more than 68 million people in Germany, and its score is queried for everything from rental applications and bank accounts to mobile phone contracts, internet deals, buy-now-pay-later checkouts — and yes, even gym memberships.

For newcomers the record usually starts when you register your address (Anmeldung) and open your first German contracts: a bank account, a phone plan, an internet connection. Each contract partner reports the account to SCHUFA — including whether you pay on time. The law is identical for everyone: SCHUFA stores no income data, and storing sensitive data such as religion or origin is strictly forbidden (Art. 9 GDPR).

The March 2026 reform: what actually changed

On 17 March 2026, after years of criticism of the opaque "black box" and landmark EU court rulings (CJEU C-634/21 and C-203/22, holding that decisive credit scoring counts as automated decision-making under Art. 22 GDPR), SCHUFA switched on a new scoring model — its "Next Generation Scoring":

  • 12 clearly named criteria instead of roughly 250 hidden data attributes.
  • One score for all industries — replacing the six industry-specific scores plus the old base score.
  • A 100–999 point scale instead of the old percentage (50%–97.5%). Each criterion carries published maximum points, summing to 999.
  • Free score viewing — consumers can see their score online after a one-time registration for a free SCHUFA account.
  • Shorter retention of negative data — including aligning insolvency data retention with the six-month public availability period required by German law. The old score is to be fully abolished by end of 2028.

Why newcomers start low — and why that is normal

A fresh arrival typically scores modestly, and none of it is personal. With a new address (criterion 4), a brand-new bank account (6), no credit card history (5), and several telecom inquiries in the first weeks (3), you are mathematically "thin file". A model citizen with no credit history at all can land around the middle of the scale — that is how the system treats people it knows little about, not a judgment on you.

What actually builds your score is time plus reliability: pay every bill on time (264 points ride on this), don't stack phone-contract applications into one week, keep your oldest account open, and let your address age. Avoid signing up for services you don't need — each one is a data point.

Get your free data copy: Datenkopie (nach Art. 15 DS-GVO)

You have a legal right to see everything SCHUFA stores about you — at least one free copy per year (Art. 15 GDPR / §34 BDSG). SCHUFA calls it Datenkopie. It lists your personal data, all stored contracts, where the information came from, and who received it in the last twelve months. Note: landlords usually want the paid SCHUFA-BonitätsAuskunft; the free Datenkopie is for your own eyes.

1Go to SCHUFA's website (meineSCHUFA) and find the section “Datenkopie (nach Art. 15 DS-GVO)” — click through the application (“Jetzt beantragen”).
2Fill in your personal details (name, date of birth, current address).
3Upload proof of identity: a scan of your passport or ID card plus your Meldebescheinigung (registration certificate).
4Submit. The paper copy is mailed to your registered address within days (allow one to two weeks).
5Read it carefully: check for wrong addresses, debts you already paid that still show as open, and accounts that are not yours. Dispute errors in writing to SCHUFA, with proof attached — incorrect entries can be corrected or deleted under data-protection law.

Five habits that protect your score

  1. Pay everything on time, always. Payment disruptions carry 264 of 999 points — more than all other criteria combined weigh per point. Set up direct debits (Lastschrift) for recurring bills.
  2. Stagger applications. Don't open a bank account, apply for a credit card and sign three phone contracts in the same week. Spread inquiries across months.
  3. Keep your oldest account. Closing your first German account resets the age clock. Keep it, even with a small balance.
  4. Check your data yearly. Order the free Datenkopie every year; a majority of Germans have never looked at their own score, and small errors drag scores down silently.
  5. Think twice before guarantees. A Bürgschaft for someone else's loan lands on your record and can cost you a flat — vouch only if you can afford to lose it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a SCHUFA score and who needs one in Germany?

SCHUFA is Germany's largest private credit bureau, with data on over 68 million people. Landlords, banks, phone and internet providers routinely check your record before signing a contract. The score runs from 100 to 999 and estimates how reliably you pay your bills.

What changed with the SCHUFA score in March 2026?

On 17 March 2026 the opaque percentage system was replaced by a transparent model: 12 named criteria, one score for all industries, a 100–999 point scale, free online score viewing after one-time registration, and shorter retention of negative data. The old score will be fully abolished by end of 2028.

What are the 12 SCHUFA scoring criteria?

Payment disruptions (264 pts), bank/credit-card applications in the last 12 months (117), non-banking inquiries in the last 12 months (99), address age (94), oldest credit card (81), oldest bank contract (69), new installment loans in the last 12 months (66), longest remaining loan term (61), mortgage (55), identity verification (38), newest credit line (36), credit status (19) — 999 points total.

I just arrived in Germany — do I already have a SCHUFA score?

A record is usually created when you register your address and open your first contracts. Newcomers start with a "thin file" — new address, new account, several telecom inquiries — so the first score is typically modest. That is normal; it grows with reliable payment history. SCHUFA stores no income data and no sensitive data like religion or origin.

How do I get my free SCHUFA data copy (Datenkopie)?

On SCHUFA's website (meineSCHUFA), find “Datenkopie (nach Art. 15 DS-GVO)” and apply: fill in your details, upload your passport/ID plus your Meldebescheinigung, and the paper copy is mailed to your address within days. It shows all stored data, its sources, and who received it in the last twelve months.

Is this simulator my real SCHUFA score?

No — it is an educational walkthrough. Your answers are mapped onto the 12 published criteria using openly stated assumptions (green keeps full points, yellow 60%, red 25%), because SCHUFA does not publish its internal sub-bands. Use it to see which factors cost you points; your real score only appears in your free SCHUFA account.
