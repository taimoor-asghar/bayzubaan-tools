Why the first 30 days have a fixed order

German administration is a chain, not a pile: each step unlocks the next. The Anmeldung (address registration) comes first because the Meldebescheinigung you receive on the spot is the key to almost everything else — bank accounts, your employer, your health insurer and, for non-EU citizens, the immigration office. Your tax ID (Steuer-ID) is issued automatically after the Anmeldung and mailed to you; your social security number arrives after your employer registers you; and the Beitragsservice learns your address automatically and writes to you. Plan in this order and nothing blocks anything else.

The paperwork chain, step by step

  1. Before you fly: check your passport's expiry date and make scans of every document; confirm health insurance for the first days (travel or incoming insurance) so you land covered.
  2. Day 1: get a German SIM card (you will need a local number for appointments and banking), and book your Anmeldung appointment the moment you have signed the lease — in big cities the next free slot can be weeks away.
  3. At the Bürgeramt (by day 14): bring passport (original), the signed Wohnungsgeberbestätigung and the completed registration form. You walk out with the Meldebescheinigung.
  4. With the Meldebescheinigung: open your bank account, hand a copy to your employer and to your health insurer. Your employer registers you for social insurance — that registration is what produces your Sozialversicherungsnummer, mailed to you afterwards.
  5. Watch the mailbox: three letters arrive on their own — the Steuer-ID from the tax office (2–4 weeks after the Anmeldung), the Sozialversicherungsnummer from the pension insurance, and the Beitragsservice letter asking you to register for the broadcasting fee.
  6. Non-EU track: contact the Ausländerbehörde in your first week — appointment queues are long, so do not wait until day 89. Citizens of the USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, New Zealand and Israel may enter visa-free and apply for the residence permit after arrival; everyone else needs their visa sorted before travel. Once you have applied, your stay counts as legal until a decision is made (§81 (3) AufenthG).

The three letters coming to your mailbox

Newcomers often panic at official-looking post. Here is what to expect so you can tell the routine from the urgent:

  • The Steuer-ID letter (from the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern): your permanent 11-digit tax ID. Give it to your employer; without it they must withhold tax at the highest class. Keep the letter — the number never changes.
  • The Beitragsservice letter: registration for the €18.36/month broadcasting fee (Rundfunkbeitrag), one per dwelling. Register online when it arrives, or apply for an exemption if you receive benefits — see our Rundfunkbeitrag Befreiungschecker.
  • The Sozialversicherungsausweis letter: proof of your social security number, issued after your employer's registration. You need the number for every future job in Germany.

Only a letter from the Ausländerbehörde (immigration office) or the Finanzamt (tax office) ever demands urgent action — everything else can wait for its natural place in the timeline above.

Situation extras worth knowing

  • Students: get your Immatrikulationsbescheinigung (enrolment certificate) ready — you need it for student health insurance and your semester ticket. See our Krankenkasse Finder for the 2026 student rates.
  • Workers: your employer cannot run payroll without your tax ID, health insurance details and bank IBAN — have all three ready in week one.
  • Families: register the whole household in one appointment — bring the children's birth certificates and your marriage certificate (originals).
  • Jobseekers: health insurance is mandatory even without a job; sort an incoming or voluntary policy in your first week and keep proof of funds ready for the immigration office.
  • Shared flat / sublet: the main tenant (Hauptmieter) is your legal Wohnungsgeber and signs your form — arrange this before the appointment, not at the counter. Generate your document list with our Anmeldung Checklist Generator.

Frequently asked questions

Does the 14-day Anmeldung deadline apply if I am staying in temporary housing?

Yes. Under §17 (1) BMG the 14-day clock starts on the day you actually move in, even temporarily. What matters is that the flat's landlord or main tenant can sign your Wohnungsgeberbestätigung — book a Bürgeramt appointment as soon as you know where you will be sleeping.

When does health insurance become mandatory in Germany?

From day one: have valid coverage from the moment you arrive and register — do not wait until you find a job. Employees give their insurance details to their employer before the first payroll, since the employer registers them for social insurance.

How do I get my German tax ID (Steuer-ID)?

You do not apply separately. After your Anmeldung the registration office transmits your data to the Federal Central Tax Office, which mails your 11-digit Steuer-ID to your registered address — usually 2–4 weeks after registration.

I am not an EU citizen — when must I visit the immigration office?

Apply for your residence permit at the Ausländerbehörde within the first 90 days, or before your visa expires — whichever comes first. Citizens of the USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, New Zealand and Israel may enter visa-free and apply after arrival. Once you have applied, your stay counts as legal until a decision is made (§81 (3) AufenthG).

What is the Sozialversicherungsnummer, and when do I receive it?

Your German social security number. After your employer registers you for social insurance (usually via your health insurer), it is mailed to you by post — typically within a few weeks of starting work. Keep the letter; you need the number for every future job.

Why did I receive a letter from the Beitragsservice after registering my address?

Because registration data is transmitted automatically — every new address is reported to the broadcasting fee office, which asks you to register and pay €18.36 per month per dwelling. This is normal, not a scam: register when the letter arrives, or apply for an exemption if you qualify.
